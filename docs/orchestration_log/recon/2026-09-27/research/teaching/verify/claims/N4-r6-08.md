## Claim as surveyed
"Students judged their own comprehension of texts in a domain where they had substantial coursework (physics or music theory) versus one where they had little; domain expertise was INVERSELY related to WITHIN-domain calibration (more expert students were relatively worse at judging whether they had understood a specific text), while calibration ACROSS domains was consistently good -- explained by a 'self-classification' strategy where confidence tracks 'am I generally an expert in this domain' rather than 'did I understand this specific text.'"
Source: 10.3758/bf03197714 (Glenberg & Epstein, 1987, Memory & Cognition — "Inexpert calibration of comprehension")

## Primary text (quote, location)
Abstract (only text obtained): "Students with a wide range of course work in physics or music theory read expositions in both domains... Two results of most interest were that (1) expertise in a domain was inversely related to calibration and (2) subjects were well calibrated across domains. Both of these results can be accommodated by a self-classification strategy: Confidence judgments are based on self-classification as expert or nonexpert in the domain of the text, rather than on an assessment of the degree to which the text was comprehended."
