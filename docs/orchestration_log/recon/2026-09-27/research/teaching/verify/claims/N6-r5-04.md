# N6-r5-04 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
After a within-session interruption, reaction time on a sequential motor task was slowest on the very first action taken upon resuming (significantly slower than the pre-interruption baseline: t(24)=8.48, p<.001, d=2.07) and recovered progressively over the next several actions, with the 3rd and 4th post-interruption actions significantly faster than the 1st and 2nd — resumption cost is real but resolves within seconds, not a lasting session-long deficit. DOI 10.1002/acp.4126.

## Primary text (quote, location)
Same full text as N6-r5-03 (OUKA repository, author's accepted manuscript). Experiment 1 results, p.20-21:

> "Multiple comparisons tests demonstrated that reaction times for A1 and A2 were longer than those for B1 (A1 > B1: t(24) = 8.48, p <.001, d = 2.07; A2 > B1: t(24) = 3.75, p =.005, d = 0.99). This result indicates that the reaction time was longer post-interruption compared with pre-interruption. Additionally, the reaction times of A3 and A4 were shorter than those of A1 and A2 (A3 < A1: t(24) = 7.24, p <.001, d = 1.92; A4 < A1: t(24) = 9.29, p <.001, d = 2.25; A3 < A2: t(24) = 4.22, p =.002, d = 0.88; A4 < A2: t(24) = 4.29, p =.002, d = 1.14). This result suggests that the response becomes progressively faster, indicating that there is a recovery process over time following the interruption."

(B1 = last button before interruption; A1-A4 = 1st-4th button after interruption.) The t(24)=8.48, p<.001, d=2.07 statistic and the "3rd/4th faster than 1st/2nd" pattern both match the claim verbatim.
