## Claim as surveyed
"Across a semester-length course, higher-performing students calibrated their own exam-performance judgments accurately without any intervention and did not improve further; lower-performing students were markedly less accurate, and only an extrinsic-incentive condition (not reflection alone) produced a significant increase in their calibration accuracy."
Source: 10.1007/s11409-008-9021-5 (Hacker, Bol & Bahbahani — "Explaining calibration accuracy in classroom contexts: the effects of incentives, reflection, and explanatory style")

## Primary text (quote, location)
Abstract (only text obtained): "A 2 × 2 quasi-experimental design was used to investigate the impact of extrinsic incentives and reflection on students' calibration of exam performance... Participants were 137 college students enrolled in an educational psychology course. Results differed as a function of exam performance. Higher-performing students were very accurate in their calibration and did not show significant improvements across a semester-length course... Lower-performing students, however, were less accurate in their calibration, and students in the incentives condition showed significant increases in calibration."
