# N2-r5-05 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
"Across 54 studies / 2,068 participants, high-CI (randomized/interleaved) practice produced a medium-to-large benefit on delayed retention of a motor skill versus low-CI (blocked) practice, significant under two different pooling models." (DOI 10.1038/s41598-024-65753-3)

## Primary text (quote, location)
Full text fetched (PMC11237090, PMID 38987617; Czyż, Wójcik, Solarská & Kiper, *Scientific Reports* 2024).

Abstract: "We screened full texts of 294 studies, of which 54 were included in the meta-analysis... According to both analyses, high CI has a medium beneficial effect on the whole population. These effects were statistically significant."

Results (three-level mixed model, whole population): pooled SMD reported as medium and significant; Results (random-effects model with averaged SMDs): pooled SMD likewise significant. Method section states 1210 laboratory participants (89 effect sizes) + 858 applied-setting participants (105 effect sizes) across the 54-study pool.

## Design facts

| field | value |
|---|---|
| design | PRISMA + PROSPERO-registered (CRD42021228267) systematic review and meta-analysis; two pooling models (3-level mixed model; random-effects on averaged SMDs) |
| n | 54 studies in the meta-analysis; 294 full texts screened; ~2,068 total participants across included studies |
| population | healthy participants across young (<18), adult (18–60) and older-adult (>60) age bands, novice and experienced, mixed motor tasks |
| instrument | standardized mean difference (Hedges' g / SMD) on retention-test performance (task-specific: accuracy, time, error scores etc.) |
| delay-to-test | retention tests at ≥24h only (immediate-retention studies were excluded from the meta-analysis and reported narratively instead); constituent delays range 24h to several weeks |

## Replications & critics
This paper's own Introduction directly disputes an existing rival meta-analysis (Ammar et al. 2023, "the myth of contextual interference..."), calling it methodologically flawed (omitted EBSCO database, no preregistration). A citing paper, Czyż et al. 2024 (`10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1377122`, companion transfer meta-analysis by the same lab), makes the same critique with numbers (see N2-r5-09). A further citing paper, `10.1007/s10648-025-10043-1` ("Advancing Contextual Interference: Addressing Methodological Debates..."), 2025, is a direct methodological-debate follow-up on this exact literature (title only checked via OpenAlex citation graph; not separately read this round).
