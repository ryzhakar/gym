## Claim as surveyed
"Giving students real-time feedback on their help-seeking behavior (a 'Help Tutor' agent) made students use on-demand hints more deliberately, and this behavior change persisted after the feedback was withdrawn, but it did not by itself improve domain-level learning outcomes." ## Primary text (quote, location)
Publisher abstract, resolved via Semantic Scholar Graph API keyed to this exact DOI (paperId 351a369786eb24aba0574f0361cd6126f18a2a33):

> "In a classroom study, feedback on help seeking helped students to use on-demand help more deliberately, even after the feedback was no longer given, but not to achieve better learning outcomes."

Verbatim match to the surveyed claim. This is the review paper's own abstract, not a WebSearch synthesis — an improvement over the survey round's provenance note.

The review paper synthesizes an earlier primary study. Resolved via Crossref bibliographic search: Roll, Aleven, McLaren, Koedinger, "Improving students' help-seeking skills using metacognitive feedback in an intelligent tutoring system," *Learning and Instruction*, 2011, DOI 10.1016/j.learninstruc.2010.07.004 (`update-to`: none, `is-referenced-by-count`: 283, no retraction). Its own abstract (Semantic Scholar, same DOI-keyed lookup):

> "Study 1, with 58 students, found that the real-time assessment of students' help-seeking behavior correlated with other independent measures of help seeking, and that the Help Tutor improved students' help-seeking behavior... Study 2, with 67 students, evaluated more elaborated support... The study replicated the effect found in Study 1. It was also found that the improved help-seeking skills transferred to learning new domain-level content during the month following the intervention, while the help-seeking support was no longer in effect."

Note: this 2011 abstract reports skill-behavior transfer to new content over the following month — not the same as a domain-level learning-outcome gain. The 2016 review's own abstract states plainly that the goal of better domain-level learning outcomes was not met. The two statements are compatible (behavior generalized; achievement did not improve) but the surveyor could not fetch either full text to confirm the exact instrument distinguishing "help-seeking behavior applied to new content" from "domain-level learning outcome."
