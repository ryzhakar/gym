# N4-r2-06 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
The 12-item tracing/code-completion instrument this multi-institution study built specifically to diagnose the reasoning-chain-vs-concept distinction (claim N4-r2-05) had a Cronbach's alpha of 0.75 on its own data -- just below the 0.80 conventional reliability threshold -- illustrating that even a carefully constructed, purpose-built diagnostic instrument for this population falls short of standard reliability.

Attributed to DOI 10.1145/1041624.1041673 (same source as N4-r2-05).

## Primary text (quote, location)
Same PDF as N4-r2-05: `https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/bitstream/10453/4126/3/2004000904.pdf`.

§3.7 Reliability, p.131 (SIGCSE Bulletin pagination), p.9 of PDF:

> "We computed Cronbach's coefficient alpha (Ebel and Frisbie, 1986), which provides the average correlation between scores of all possible two-group partitions of the questions. Using the data for students who were given all 12 MCQs (N=556), we obtained a value of 0.75. By convention, a value of 0.80 or higher is considered 'reliable.' In the set of 12 MCQs used in this study, Questions 6, 8, and 12 (one quarter of the complete set of questions) stand out as being relatively difficult questions. Therefore it is not surprising that the reliability of the complete set of 12 MCQs falls a little below the conventional threshold of 0.80."

## Design facts

| field | value |
|---|---|
| design | same multi-institution observational survey as N4-r2-05; reliability computed post hoc on the pooled instrument responses |
| n | 556 (students who completed all 12 MCQs) |
| population | CS1 students, seven countries, multiple institutions |
| instrument | the same 12-item tracing/code-completion MCQ set; alpha computed via all possible two-group split-half partitions |
| delay-to-test | n/a — psychometric property of one administration, not a repeated-measures design |

Note: the instrument was not built "specifically to diagnose the reasoning-chain-vs-concept distinction" as an a priori design goal — the paper frames the 12 MCQs as a general tracing/code-completion instrument, and the reasoning-chain-vs-concept interpretation (§3.9) is a post hoc discussion of the quartile results, not the instrument's stated design purpose. This is a minor mischaracterization in the claim's framing; the 0.75 alpha figure itself is exact.

## Status: VERIFIED

The 0.75 alpha figure, N=556, and the authors' own framing ("falls a little below the conventional threshold of 0.80") are quoted verbatim and match exactly. Sole caveat: the claim's premise that the instrument was "built specifically to diagnose" the reasoning-chain-vs-concept distinction overstates the paper's own framing (that distinction is a post hoc interpretation of results, not the instrument's design goal) — noted above, does not affect the accuracy of the 0.75 figure itself.
