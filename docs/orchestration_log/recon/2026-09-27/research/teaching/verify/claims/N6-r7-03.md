# N6-r7-03 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
Pooling 24 studies (31 effect sizes) that used separate scales for curiosity and interest, a meta-analysis found only a moderate correlation between the two constructs (r=.53) with extremely high heterogeneity across studies — meaning how strongly curiosity and interest track each other in a given sample depends heavily on how each was operationalised, not on a fixed underlying relationship. DOI 10.31234/osf.io/wfprn (PsyArXiv preprint).

## Primary text (quote, location)
Full text obtained this round (not read in the survey round, which logged abstract-only): the OSF API (`api.figshare`-style `api.osf.io/v2/preprints/wfprn/`) links a direct PDF download (`osf.io/download/wfprn/`), fetched and converted (75-page preprint). Results section:

> "Based on our inclusion criteria, 24 studies with 31 independent samples, involving over 4,817 participants and 31 correlations between curiosity and interest were included in the final analysis... Overall, the relation between curiosity and interest was moderate-to-high and significant, r = .53, p < .001, 95% CI [.38, .66]. However, there is a very large heterogeneity among the correlations, I2 = 96.9%, Q (30) = 954.51, p < .001; r ranges from -.32 to .98."

Every number in the claim (24 studies, 31 effect sizes, r=.53, extremely high heterogeneity) is confirmed, with additional precision available in the full text beyond the abstract (I²=96.9%, Q(30)=954.51, individual-study r range -.32 to .98).
