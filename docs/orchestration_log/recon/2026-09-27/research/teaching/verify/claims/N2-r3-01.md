# N2-r3-01 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
"Problem-based/project-style practice outperformed conventional instruction on long-term retention and on measures of skill development, across the meta-analyses this synthesis compared." (Strobel & van Barneveld 2009, DOI 10.7771/1541-5015.1046)

## Primary text (quote, location)
Full text read (open access, Purdue e-Pubs, 17pp). Results and Discussion section:

"Knowledge assessment that focused on long-term knowledge retention, described by Albanese and Mitchell (1993) as a comparison of immediate post-course results and results of the same test applied after a period of between 12 weeks to 2 years, returned effect sizes that consistently favored PBL. Dochy et al. (2003) looked only at whether a retention period existed and compared knowledge outcomes on the basis of retention period or no retention period. Long-term knowledge retention favored PBL." (p.54)

"The Performance or skill-based assessment category included observations with clinical ratings ... and case analysis measures. Clinical ratings favored PBL. The case analysis sub-category ... also favored PBL." (p.54)

Conclusion (p.55): "PBL instruction was effective when it came to long-term retention and performance improvement."

## Design facts (table)

| fact | value |
|---|---|
| design | meta-synthesis (qualitative, narrative synthesis method per Bair 1999) of 8 prior meta-analyses/systematic reviews — not itself a pooled quantitative meta-analysis; authors explicitly rejected a "meta-meta-analysis" of effect sizes in favor of qualitative synthesis |
| n / studies pooled | 8 meta-analyses/systematic reviews (Albanese & Mitchell 1993; Vernon & Blake 1993; Berkson 1993; Kalaian, Mullan & Kasim 1999; Colliver 2000; Dochy et al. 2003; Newman 2003; Gijbels et al. 2005), spanning primary studies from 1970–2000 |
| population | overwhelmingly medical/health-professional education; paper states outright "the vast majority of research on the effectiveness of PBL has been conducted in the training of professionals in the field of medicine" |
| instrument | mixed: standardized board exams (NBME 1/2), free recall, short-answer, clinical ratings, case analyses, oral exams |
| delay-to-test | long-term retention band = 12 weeks to 2 years post-course (per Albanese & Mitchell subset); Dochy et al. used a binary retention-period/no-retention-period split, no fixed delay given |

Caveat on scope: only 2 of the 8 source studies (Albanese & Mitchell 1993; Dochy et al. 2003) are named as explicitly reporting the long-term-retention breakdown; both favored PBL. The "across the meta-analyses" framing in the claim reflects the synthesis authors' own generalized conclusion, not a tally in which all 8 sources independently measured long-term retention.
