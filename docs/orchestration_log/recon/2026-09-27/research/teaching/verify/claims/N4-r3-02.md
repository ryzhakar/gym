# N4-r3-02 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
"Standard Bayesian Knowledge Tracing, the dominant diagnosis model this literature builds on, structurally assumes a skill once learned is never forgotten -- a limitation the newer neural diagnosis model does not remove but the paper flags explicitly." (arxiv.org/abs/1506.05908)

## ID resolution
Same paper as N4-r3-01: `arxiv.org/abs/1506.05908`, "Deep Knowledge Tracing," Piech et al. 2015 — confirmed via arXiv API (see N4-r3-01 for full resolution detail).

## Primary text (quote, location)
Full text fetched directly (`arxiv.org/pdf/1506.05908v1`, 13 pp.).

§2.1 "Bayesian Knowledge Tracing," p.2: "Bayesian Knowledge Tracing (BKT) is the most popular approach for building temporal models of student learning... The formulation of the original model assumed that once a skill is learned it is never forgotten. Recent extensions to this model include contextualization of guessing and slipping estimates [7], estimating prior knowledge for individual learners [33], and estimating problem difficulty [23]."

This quote confirms: (a) BKT is presented as the dominant/most-popular prior model this paper's literature builds on ("the most popular approach"), and (b) the "never forgotten" assumption is attributed to "the original model," with the paper immediately listing extensions that address other limitations (guessing/slipping, prior knowledge, difficulty) — notably, **no extension listed addresses forgetting**.

§7 "Discussion," p.9 (near the end): "Further investigations could incorporate other features as inputs (such as time taken), explore other educational impacts (such as hint generation, dropout prediction), and validate hypotheses posed in education literature (such as spaced repetition, modeling how students forget)."

This is the paper's own forward-looking statement, listing "modeling how students forget" as **future work**, not something the current DKT model does. This is the paper's implicit acknowledgment that DKT, as presented in this paper, does not model forgetting either — the claim's "does not remove [the limitation]" is a reasonable, source-supported inference from this Discussion sentence, though the paper does not state in so many words "DKT still assumes skills are never forgotten." The "flags explicitly" half of the claim is accurate only insofar as the paper explicitly names forgetting-modeling as unaddressed/future work; it is not an explicit statement that DKT inherits BKT's specific structural assumption.

Same relevant critique as N4-r3-01: Khajah, Lindsey & Mozer, "How deep is knowledge tracing?" (arXiv:1604.02416, 2016), confirmed resolving to that title/authors. ## Status: VERIFIED

The directly-quoted sentence ("once a skill is learned it is never forgotten," attributed to BKT) is exact and correctly located (§2.1, p.2). The second half of the claim (DKT does not remove this limitation, and the paper flags it) is a fair but inferential reading of the Discussion's future-work sentence about "modeling how students forget" (p.9) — the paper does not explicitly re-state the forgetting assumption in connection with DKT itself. S is downgraded from 3 to 1 because this claim is not an empirical result of the paper, unlike N4-r3-01.
