## Replications & critics

OpenAlex work W2023276160, cited_by_count=1,515 (too large to screen exhaustively; filtered by title keywords replication/reanalysis/comment/meta-analysis/critique, 20 hits). Most relevant: **Association between Surgical Technical Skills and Clinical Outcomes: A Systematic Literature Review and Meta-Analysis** (Ma et al., *JSLS* 2023, DOI 10.4293/jsls.2022.00076) — a meta-analysis pooling 7 eligible skill-outcome studies (151 surgeons, 17,932 procedures) across pancreaticoduodenectomy, gastric bypass, laparoscopic gastrectomy, prostatectomy, colorectal, hemicolectomy — gastric bypass eligibility criterion means this Birkmeyer cohort is a plausible/likely included study (not individually confirmed by reading its included-studies table, which was not accessible). Pooled findings, quoted from its abstract:

> "Meta-analytic pooling identified significant associations between the highest vs. lowest quartile of surgical skill and reoperation (OR: 0.44...), hemorrhage (OR: 0.66...), obstruction (OR: 0.33...), and any medical complication (OR: 0.23...). Nonsignificant inverse associations were noted between skill and readmission, emergency department visit, **mortality**, leak, infection, venous thromboembolism, and cardiac and pulmonary complications."

This is a partial non-replication at the mortality endpoint specifically: Birkmeyer's single-study mortality difference (0.26% vs 0.05%, P=0.01) does not hold as statistically significant once pooled with the broader literature, even as the general skill→complication direction replicates across other endpoints (reoperation, hemorrhage, obstruction, overall complications).
