# N2-r1-03 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
The same interleaving intervention required no teacher training to implement, and teachers who ran it later said they supported interleaved practice, before seeing their own results.

## Primary text (quote, location)
Full text read: same source as N2-r1-02 — Rohrer, Dedrick, Hartwig & Cheung (2020), *Journal of Educational Psychology* 112(1), 40–52, DOI 10.1037/edu0000367. Same PDF (Wayback-archived mirror of Rohrer's author page, verified against Crossref metadata).

Abstract (p.40):
> "Teachers were able to implement the intervention without training, and they later expressed support for interleaved practice in an anonymous survey they completed before they knew the results of the study."

"Feasibility" discussion (p.49):
> "The participating teachers were able to incorporate interleaved mathematics practice in their classrooms without training or support, and most reported that the intervention is effective and easy to use."

"Teacher Survey" method section (p.44, unnumbered page before Results):
> "Several weeks after the test, the second author hand-delivered to each teacher a 23-item paper-and-pencil survey and a stamped envelope addressed to the author. Each teacher was asked to anonymously complete the survey and return it by mail. All teachers returned the survey, and only then did we inform them of the results and purpose of the study."

Results, "Teacher Survey" (p.48): "Teachers agreed (or strongly agreed) that interleaved practice is a good way to improve students' scores on unit exams (14) and final exams (14)..." — 14–15 of 15 teachers endorsing each favorable item.

Quote matches claim closely; note the survey's own wording is "most reported" effective/easy to use (not unanimous), and support is one item among several on a 23-item survey, not a single up/down verdict — the claim's plain-language gloss is accurate but slightly compresses this nuance.

## Design facts (table)

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Design | Anonymous 23-item paper survey embedded in the same cluster-RCT as N2-r1-02, administered after the intervention period but before results/purpose were disclosed to teachers |
| n | 15 teachers (all returned the survey; 100% response) |
| Population | 7th-grade mathematics teachers, same US public school district as N2-r1-02 |
| Instrument | Self-report Likert-style survey items (perceived difficulty, time cost, effectiveness, appropriateness for low/high achievers), not a performance measure |
| Delay-to-test | Survey delivered "several weeks after the test" (i.e., after the one-month-delayed unannounced student test), before teachers were told results — no unaided-performance delay applies since this is a satisfaction/feasibility measure |

## Replications & critics
Same citing-works search as N2-r1-02 (OpenAlex rate-limited; Crossref fallback used). No third-party study replicating or disputing the teacher-feasibility/support finding specifically was found. Two same-authors follow-ons touch teacher perceptions but are distinct studies, not replications of this survey: "Preservice and Middle School Mathematics Teachers' Perceptions of Interleaved Mathematics Practice" (2020, DOI 10.3102/1578165) and "Students' Perceptions of Interleaved Mathematics Practice: Potential Barriers to Implementation" (2021, DOI 10.3102/1683719) — both extend the question to other populations (preservice teachers, students) rather than confirming or refuting this specific teacher cohort's reported ease-of-use.

## Grade (S R O, changed from survey? why)
S=3, R=1, O=0 — unchanged from survey. Survey data collected within the same controlled RCT as N2-r1-02, with a defined design and full response (S=3, not a bare practitioner anecdote); population is 7th-grade classroom teachers (R=1, K-12 context); outcome is self-reported perception/satisfaction, not a performance measure (O=0) — this claim is about implementation feasibility and support, and per the grading rules an O=0 claim never enters a recommendation set regardless of S.
