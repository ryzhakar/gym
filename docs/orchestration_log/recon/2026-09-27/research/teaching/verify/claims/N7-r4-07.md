# N7-r4-07 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
In a 2x2 between-subjects quasi-experiment (n=94 novice university programming students), adding self-explanation training and/or self-explanation prompts on top of ChatGPT-supported practice produced no significant main or interaction effect on programming (code-writing) gain scores, despite overall pre-to-post learning occurring in all conditions — a known human-tutoring-effective technique failed to add benefit on the LLM substrate. DOI 10.22215/etd/2026-17196.

## Primary text (quote, location)
Full text read (57-page Carleton University master's thesis PDF, obtained via `https://hdl.handle.net/20.500.14718/45353` → `https://carleton.scholaris.ca/bitstreams/e6422f18-39ee-43f0-b938-88e65a37cfd9/download`).

Section 5.2, thesis internal p.33 (PDF p.41):

> "A 2 (prompting: present, absent) × 2 (training: present, absent) between-subjects ANOVA was conducted with programming gain scores as the dependent variable. There was no significant main effect of prompting, F(1, 90) = 0.04, p = .839, η² < .001, and no significant main effect of training, F(1, 90) = 0.78, p = .381, η² = .008. The prompting × training interaction was also not significant, F(1, 90) = 1.05, p = .309, η² = .011."

Overall pre-to-post learning, abstract (PDF p.1):

> "...indicating overall learning. However, gain-score analyses showed no [significant effects of training/prompting]"
