# N2-r1-10 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed

"Pooling 53 studies/166 comparisons (12,000+ participants), letting learners attempt problem solving before instruction beat instruction-before-problem-solving on average (Hedge's g=0.36), stronger under high-fidelity productive-failure design (g=0.37-0.58), but the advantage reversed toward instruction-first for grades 2-5 and for domain-general-skill outcomes." (DOI 10.3102/00346543211019105 — same source as N3-r1-02)

## Primary text (quote, location)

Full text: same publisher PDF already retrieved for N3-r1-02, via ETH Zurich Research Collection (open bitstream, published version, https://www.research-collection.ethz.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/f6c98c41-ec4b-4996-b677-97fa3c997cf9/content). Sinha, T., & Kapur, M. (2021), *Review of Educational Research*, 91(5), 761–798.

Abstract / p. 761:
> "we report evidence from a meta-analysis of 53 studies with 166 comparisons that compared PS-I with I-PS design. Our results showed a significant, moderate effect in favor of PS-I (Hedge's g 0.36 [95% confidence interval 0.20; 0.51]). The effects were even stronger (Hedge's g ranging between 0.37 and 0.58) when PS-I was implemented with high fidelity to the principles of Productive Failure (PF)... Contrasting trends were, however, observed for younger age learners (second to fifth graders) and for the learning of domain-general skills, for which effect sizes favored I-PS."

Pooled effect confirmed again in Results (p. 776):
> "Results for Research Question 1 suggested that overall, the SMD or effect size for conceptual knowledge and transfer was moderate (Hedge's g = 0.36, p < .0001, 95% CI [0.20, 0.51]), and in favor of PS-I."

Grade 2–5 reversal (Table 4, p. 779): "Age range (grade level) — 2nd to 5th graders (−0.09 [−0.92, −0.16])."
Domain-general skills reversal (Table 5): "Domain-general skills (−0.17 [−1.11, −0.02])."

**"12,000+ participants" — NOT confirmed in primary text.** I searched the full extracted text (abstract, method, Table 1's descriptive breakdown of 166 comparisons, results, discussion) for any total-participant tally (`grep` for `N =`, `participants`, `sample size`, and any 5-digit/comma-grouped counts) and found no statement of total sample size across the 53 studies/166 comparisons anywhere in the article body. Table 1 reports comparison counts by category (geography, grade, subject), not participant Ns. The "12,000+ participants" figure does not appear in this paper as read; its likely origin is secondary press coverage (e.g., the ETH news release / phys.org piece on this study), not the peer-reviewed text itself. This is flagged, not treated as refuting the rest of the claim.

## Design facts

- Design: three-level random-effects meta-analysis, 53 studies, 166 (quasi-)experimental comparisons; PRISMA-flowchart selection from 1,212 initially identified articles down to 45 articles (908 after dedup, 45 met inclusion criteria 2 and 3).
- Population: mixed grade levels — 2nd–5th graders (15.1% of comparisons), 6th–10th graders (45.2%), undergraduates (36.7%), postgraduates/professionals (3%). Not adult-only.
- Instrument: conceptual knowledge, transfer, procedural knowledge, "clubbed" outcome measures.
- Delay-to-test: not coded as a moderator; outcome-type (conceptual/transfer/procedural), not delay interval, is what's separated.
- Total participant count: unreported in the body text (see caveat above).

## Grade (S R O, changed from survey? why)

S=4, R=2, O=1 — unchanged from survey. S=4: three-level meta-analysis with heterogeneity (Q[165]=295.91) and multiple moderator analyses. R=2: population spans 2nd grade through professionals, not adult-specific. O=1 (not raised to 2, unlike N3-r1-02): this claim's headline number (g=0.36) is the pooled estimate over conceptual knowledge *and* transfer combined, not an isolated transfer-only or ≥7-day-delay figure, so it sits at immediate/mixed-outcome performance rather than meeting the O=2 transfer-task or delayed-test bar on its own terms.
