# N3-r1-02 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed

"Problem-solving-before-instruction (productive failure) beats instruction-before-problem-solving with a moderate pooled effect (Hedge's g=0.36, 95% CI 0.20-0.51) on conceptual understanding and transfer without harming procedural knowledge; the pattern reverses for 2nd-5th graders and for domain-general skills." (DOI 10.3102/00346543211019105)

## Primary text (quote, location)

Full text obtained: publisher PDF via ETH Zurich Research Collection (institutional repository copy, published version, open, bitstream `00346543211019105.pdf`, https://www.research-collection.ethz.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/f6c98c41-ec4b-4996-b677-97fa3c997cf9/content).

Sinha, T., & Kapur, M. (2021). "When Problem Solving Followed by Instruction Works: Evidence for Productive Failure." *Review of Educational Research*, 91(5), 761–798.

Pooled effect (p. 776, "Pooled Effect Size Estimates"):
> "Results for Research Question 1 suggested that overall, the SMD or effect size for conceptual knowledge and transfer was moderate (Hedge's g = 0.36, p < .0001, 95% CI [0.20, 0.51]), and in favor of PS-I."

Procedural knowledge unharmed (p. 776–777):
> "For the n = 51/166 comparisons assessing procedural knowledge, results suggested the pooled effect size for procedural knowledge to be Hedge's g = −0.03, p = .7384, 95% CI [−0.20, 0.15]."

Grade-level reversal (p. 779, "Students' Incoming Characteristics"; Table 4):
> "Age range, as assessed by students' grade level, had (marginally) significant subgroup differences for all student subcategories... The pooled effect size estimate for younger students (2nd to 5th graders) was negative, and these estimates increased (or became more positive) with the age range."
Table 4: "Age range (grade level) — 2nd to 5th graders (−0.09 [−0.92, −0.16])."

Domain-general skills reversal (Table 5; p. 1029 of extracted text / discussion section):
> "With respect to learning concepts targeted in the interventions, moderate effect sizes in favor of PS-I were observed for domain-specific subjects (e.g., math, basic sciences, medicine). For learning domain-general skills, however, the effect size was low, and in favor of I-PS."
Table 5: "Domain-general skills (−0.17 [−1.11, −0.02])."

## Design facts

- Design: three-level random-effects meta-analysis, 53 studies, 166 comparisons, >12,000 participants (per abstract/body); heterogeneity Q(df=165)=295.91, p<.0001, I²=42.01%.
- Population: mixed — K-12 (2nd–10th graders), undergraduates, postgraduates/professionals; not restricted to adults.
- Instrument: conceptual knowledge, transfer, procedural knowledge, and "clubbed" assessment outcomes, coded per comparison.
- Delay-to-test: not systematically coded/reported as a moderator in this meta-analysis; outcome type (conceptual/transfer/procedural) is the reported distinction, not delay interval.
