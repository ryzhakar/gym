# N7-r1-06 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed

"Pooling 4 trials (n=268), AI-augmented surgical tutoring showed only a small, low-certainty advantage over expert human instruction (OSATS mean difference 0.20) and imposed higher extraneous cognitive load; the review's authors recommend a hybrid model, not replacement of human instructors." DOI 10.1186/s12909-026-09606-9. Surveyed S4 R2 O1.

## Primary text (quote, location)

Hanna et al., "Artificial intelligence augmented tutoring vs expert instruction on learning simulated general surgical skills: a systematic review and meta-analysis," BMC Medical Education 26:952 (2026). Full text read (publisher PDF via Unpaywall; cross-checked against PMC13251200).

Abstract: "Four studies (3 RCTs and 1 pilot prospective study), encompassing a total of 268 participants, were included in the meta-analysis. AI tutoring showed a small, statistically significant improvement in expert-rated OSATS scores (MD 0.20; 95% CI, 0.01 to 0.39) with no heterogeneity (I2 = 0%) but with low certainty. The AI group reported a significantly higher extraneous cognitive load (MD 0.23; p = 0.01)."

OSATS section (Results): "The second primary outcome was learning and skill retention, as evaluated by OSATS. The meta-analysis included three studies with a total of 159 participants. The pooled results ... showed a statistically significant difference favoring the AI-tutored group, with an MD of 0.20 (95% CI, 0.01 to 0.39; p = 0.04) ... this pooled estimate was heavily weighted (79.9%) by Yang et al., a study assessed as having high risk of bias ... The certainty of this evidence was rated as low."

Cognitive load section: "The analysis of extraneous cognitive load revealed a statistically significant difference, with the AI-tutored group reporting a higher load than the expert-led group (MD = 0.23; 95% CI, 0.05 to 0.41; p = 0.01)."

Conclusion: "these findings do not support replacing human instructors with AI. Instead, the evidence supports a hybrid model" — restated in Discussion as a "hybrid 'centaur'" model.

**Nuance not in the surveyed claim:** the "4 trials, n=268" figure is the meta-analysis's total intake across all pooled outcomes (OSATS, ICEMS, cognitive-load subscales); the OSATS pooling specifically drew on only 3 of those 4 studies (n=159). The abstract itself juxtaposes the two numbers the way the claim does, so the claim reproduces the source's own (slightly loose) framing rather than introducing an error, but a reader should not take "n=268" as the OSATS comparison's own sample size.
