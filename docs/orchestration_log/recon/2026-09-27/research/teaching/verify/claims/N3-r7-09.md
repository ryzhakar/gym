## Claim as surveyed
"Gradually fading worked-example guidance into practice problems (vs. static example-problem pairs, vs. pure problem-solving) produced its largest, statistically significant advantage specifically on the delayed posttest rather than the immediate one, in adult professional (not student) trainees."
Source: 10.1080/01443410802322069 (Kissane, Kalyuga, Chandler & Sweller, 2008 — "The consequences of fading instructional guidance on delayed performance: the case of financial services training")

## Primary text (quote, location)
Abstract (only text obtained, via UNSW institutional repository metadata page): "This study compared performance, both immediately post-instruction and delayed, following instruction under different conditions: using example-problem pairs, using gradual fading of worked examples, and using pure problem-solving. The study was conducted with employees of a financial services company in a classroom environment. Results indicated that the fading condition consistently outperformed the example-problem and problem-solving conditions, and the advantage of this condition was enhanced, with statistically significant differences in performance, in delayed and transfer posttest performance."
