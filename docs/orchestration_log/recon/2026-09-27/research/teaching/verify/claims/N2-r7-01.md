# N2-r7-01 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
Reordering the same set of practice problems so consecutive items require different strategies (interleaving), versus grouping by type (blocking), produced a large delayed-test advantage on the same total material.

## Primary text (quote, location)
DOI 10.1037/edu0000367 — Rohrer, Dedrick, Hartwig & Cheung (2020), *A Randomized Controlled Trial of Interleaved Mathematics Practice*, Journal of Educational Psychology 112(1):40-52. Full text fetched (via `cache.py`, resolved a working full-text route).

Abstract: "we assessed the efficacy and feasibility of interleaved practice in a naturalistic setting with a large, diverse sample. Each of 54 7th-grade mathematics classes periodically completed interleaved or blocked assignments over a period of 4 months, and then both groups completed an interleaved review assignment. One month later, students took an unannounced test, and the interleaved group outscored the blocked group, 61% versus 38%, d = 0.83."

Results (p.44-45): "the interleaved group outscored the blocked group by a large margin... The effect size was large, Cohen's d = 0.83, 95% CI = [0.68, 0.97]... We observed a positive effect for each of the 15 teachers, ds = 0.23-1.48."

## Design facts
| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Design | Preregistered cluster RCT; 54 classes across multiple schools, randomly assigned at the class level (students nested within classes) |
| n | 787 students who completed the study (856 began, 69 [8%] withdrew or missed the test), 15 teachers, 54 classes |
| Population | 7th-grade math students (K-12, not adults) |
| Instrument | Unannounced test one month after final practice; multilevel/HLM analysis given the cluster design |
| Delay to test | 1 month (test delay equated between conditions via a shared interleaved review assignment before the test) |

Note on framing: "same total material" in the claim is accurate — the design equates practice time and problem count between conditions and varies only the sequencing/mixture of problem types, with the review assignment specifically added to equate test delay between groups.
