# N1-r3-01 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
In a school-based CPR retention meta-analysis, continuous compression-metric changes were statistically non-significant at 2-8 months while pass/fail correct-technique success rates swung from 86.9% to 12.81%, showing instrument choice (continuous vs threshold) changes the apparent retention curve.

## Primary text (quote, location)
Onbasilar et al., "Skill retention after school-based CPR training – a systematic review and meta-analysis," BMC Public Health (2025) 25:3798, DOI 10.1186/s12889-025-25024-w. Full text read (publisher PDF via Unpaywall, CC-BY).

Continuous metric (mean depth, mm), p.6-7: "Changes for mean chest compression depth Analyses of weighted mean depths using paired t-tests indicated non-significant changes in both short-term (Table 13) and long-term follow-ups (Table 14)."

Pass/fail metric (correct-depth success rate, NRSIs), Table 17, p.14-15: "Pooled Weighted Results 2839 86,90 1791 12,81 1.0859 0.4738" — text: "A large change in success rates was observed in long-term follow-ups, from 86.9% to 12.81% after 6–8 months (Table 17), but these results were not significant in formal testing due to heterogeneity (RR = 0.3223, 95% CI [0.0443; 2.3453], p = 0.263)."

## Grade (S R O, changed from survey? why)
S=4, R=1, O=2 — unchanged. S=4: systematic review/meta-analysis reporting heterogeneity. R=1: K-12 population. O=2: outcome measured at 6–8 months.

Caveat not in the survey line: the paper itself flags the 86.9%→12.81% swing as **not statistically significant** ("due to heterogeneity"). The claim does not assert the swing was significant — only that it is large in raw terms while the continuous metric stayed flat — so this is consistent, but a reader should not take "swung" to mean "significantly differed."
