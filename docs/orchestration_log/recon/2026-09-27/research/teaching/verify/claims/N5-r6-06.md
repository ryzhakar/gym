# N5-r6-06 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
The advantage of quiet-eye-trained elite golfers over controls was not just an immediate lab effect: it held in a laboratory retention/pressure test and carried onto the golf course, where the QE-trained group made 1.9 fewer putts per round across a further 10 rounds of real competition.

## Primary text (quote, location)
DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2011.00008 — same source as N5-r6-01. Full text fetched.

Abstract: "Importantly, these advantages transferred to the golf course, where QE-trained golfers made 1.9 fewer putts per round, compared to pre-training, whereas the Control group showed no change in their putting statistics."

Results, Competitive performance / Putts per round (p.5): QE-trained group mean = 27.61 (SD=1.93) vs. Control mean = 29.89 (SD=3.11) post-training [t(20)=2.11, p<0.05]; within-group QE-trained reduction pre→post [t(11)=6.18, p<0.001]; Control showed no significant change [t(9)=0.18, p=0.86]. Discussion (p.6): "the QE-trained group reduced their mean number of putts per round by 1.92 shots and holed 5% more putts from 6 to 10 ft."

## Design facts
Same study as N5-r6-01: RCT, n=22 elite golfers, competitive putting statistics recorded over 10 rounds pre-training and 10 rounds post-training (each window up to 3 months), plus a lab retention/pressure re-test in between.

## Retraction check
Same as N5-r6-01: no retraction, no update-to/relation flags.

## Replications & critics
