# N2-r1-01 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
The inter-study interval that maximizes final-test retention rises as the retention interval (delay to test) rises; spacing benefit is a ratio between study spacing and how long retention must last, not a fixed lag.

## Primary text (quote, location)
Full text read: Cepeda, Pashler, Vul, Wixted & Rohrer (2006), "Distributed Practice in Verbal Recall Tasks: A Review and Quantitative Synthesis," *Psychological Bulletin* 132(3), 354–380. DOI 10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354. PDF: https://augmentingcognition.com/assets/Cepeda2006.pdf (escholarship.org's copy, cited in the survey row, returned CloudFront 403s on every URL tried — item and direct PDF — so this mirror was used instead; same publisher PDF, verified against Crossref metadata: title, authors, journal, volume/issue/pages all match).

Abstract (p.354):
> "Analyses suggest that ISI and retention interval operate jointly to affect final-test retention; specifically, the ISI producing maximal retention increased as retention interval increased."

Body, restating the same finding with magnitudes (unnumbered page, "Discussion of absolute-lag effects" section):
> "Moreover, the optimal ISI increased as retention interval increased. For instance, at retention intervals of less than 1 min, ISIs of less than 1 min maximized retention; at retention intervals of 6 months or more, ISIs of at least 1 month maximized retention."

Quote matches claim exactly; "ratio" framing is the paper's own description of this same relationship elsewhere in the text ("optimal ratio of ISI to retention interval").

## Design facts (table)

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Design | Meta-analysis / quantitative synthesis of primary spacing-effect studies, with moderator (retention-interval × ISI) breakdowns |
| n | 839 assessments of distributed practice, 317 experiments, 184 articles; Table 6 alone reports data points across thousands of unique participants (e.g. 3,248 unique participants for one retention-interval/ISI cell) |
| Population | Mixed, mostly lab-based verbal-memory studies (list recall, paired-associate tasks); not tied to one age group or professional domain |
| Instrument | Final recall/recognition test after a fixed retention interval, compared across ISI conditions |
| Delay-to-test | Retention intervals studied range from seconds to 30–2,900 days (up to ~8 years) across the synthesized studies |
