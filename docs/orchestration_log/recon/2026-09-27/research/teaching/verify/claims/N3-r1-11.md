# N3-r1-11 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed

"In a high-school math field RCT, students given unrestricted GPT-4 access scored higher on assisted practice problems but scored lower on a subsequent unaided exam than a no-AI control group - assisted-performance gains did not carry over to unaided, retained performance." DOI 10.1073/pnas.2422633122. Surveyed S3 R1 O2.

## Primary text (quote, location)

Bastani, Bastani, Sungu, Ge, Kabakcı, Mariman, "Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning: Evidence from high school mathematics," PNAS 122(26), 2025. Full text read via PMC (PMC12232635; PDF blocked by NCBI proof-of-work challenge, HTML full text extracted instead — page/section numbers unavailable in this rendering, section names used).

Abstract: "students in the GPT Tutor (resp., GPT Base) arm perform 127% (resp., 48%) better on the practice problems compared to students in the control arm... on the exam, students in the GPT Base arm perform statistically significantly worse than students in the control arm by 17%."

Methods (Main Results section): "Each session has three contiguous parts... The second part is an assisted practice period... The third part is an unassisted evaluation, where students take a closed-book, closed-laptop exam. Importantly, each problem in the exam corresponds to a conceptually very similar practice problem from the previous part."

## Design facts

| field | value |
|---|---|
| design | RCT, classroom-randomized to 3 arms (control / GPT Base / GPT Tutor); preregistered, primary analysis = unassisted exam outcomes |
| n | ~1,000 students, ~50 classes, grades 9-11 |
| population | high school students, Turkey, Fall 2023-24 semester |
| instrument | closed-book closed-laptop exam, problems mirroring practice problems, graded by rubric |
| delay-to-test | same 90-min session, third of three contiguous parts — immediate, not ≥7-day retention |
