# N1-r7-08 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
"In the same RCT, students showed no awareness that using the ungated AI tool as a 'crutch' (copying solutions) was associated with their own subsequent unassisted-exam underperformance -- learners' self-perception of their own learning did not track the measured, unaided outcome, so self-report of felt progress cannot substitute for an unaided-performance check even when the discrepancy is large enough to be statistically significant." (DOI 10.1073/pnas.2422633122)

## Primary text (quote, location)
Full text (same source as N7-r6-01). Abstract: "we find evidence that students do not perceive any reduction in their learning or subsequent performance as a consequence of copying solutions, suggesting they are not aware of how generative AI can impede their learning." Main Results, "Student Perception": "students' own self-reported perceptions of the effects of GPT tutors on their exam performance and learning are overly optimistic. While students in the GPT Base arm performed worse on the exam (relative to the control arm), they did not perceive that they performed worse or learned less. Similarly, while students in the GPT Tutor arm did not perform better on the exam (relative to the control arm), they perceived that they performed significantly better."

Exact match — including the additional finding (not in the claim's own quote but confirming its spirit) that the GPT Tutor arm shows the mismatch running the other direction too (perceived gains that didn't materialize).

## Design facts
Same trial as N7-r6-01 — see that file. Perception measured via student self-report survey after the session, compared against actual GPT Base vs. control exam-score gap (statistically significant, per N7-r6-01).
