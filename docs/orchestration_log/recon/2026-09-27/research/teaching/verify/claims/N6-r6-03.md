## Claim as surveyed
"Interruption-resumption skill (the ability to retrieve a suspended next-step after being interrupted) transfers to a novel task pair rather than being locked to the specific task on which it was practiced, contradicting an earlier reading of the interruption literature that predicted no applied benefit from training."
Source: 10.1037/xap0000193

## Primary text (quote, location)
Abstract (only text obtained): "Evidence has also indicated that interruption practice only improves resumption for the practiced pair of primary and interrupting tasks. If this is true, then there is little applied benefit in interruption training, because the training would be unlikely to transfer beyond the training environment... The results of Experiments 1A and 1B provide evidence that interruption resumption skill transfers to another primary task when the interrupting task is either held constant or varies. Experiment 2 manipulated the primary tasks to minimize the ability to reconstruct the next step in problem solving... The results support the conclusion that resumption skill does transfer."
