# N6-r3-04 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
"Across 3 archival field studies (Google search volume for 'diet', a university gym's check-in logs, and an online goal-commitment website's records), aspirational-behaviour initiation reliably spikes right after temporal landmarks -- the start of a new week, month, year, a semester, a birthday, a holiday -- attributed to landmarks psychologically demarcating a fresh 'mental accounting period' that relegates past lapses to the prior period." (DOI 10.1287/mnsc.2014.1901)

## Primary text (quote, location)
Full text fetched: author-hosted PDF, `https://katherinemilkman.squarespace.com/s/the-fresh-start-effect.pdf` (located via Katy Milkman's faculty site, `katymilkman.com/journal-articles/the-fresh-start-effect-temporal-landmarks-motivate-aspirational-behavior`, after Unpaywall returned `is_oa: false` and Semantic Scholar returned `openAccessPdf.status: CLOSED` for this DOI).

Abstract/intro (p.1): "Across three archival field studies, we provide evidence of a 'fresh start effect.' We show that Google searches for the term 'diet' (Study 1), gym visits (Study 2), and commitments to pursue goals (Study 3) all increase following temporal landmarks (e.g., the outset of a new week, month, year, or semester; a birthday; a holiday). We propose that these landmarks demarcate the passage of time, creating many new mental accounting periods each year, which relegate past imperfections to a previous period..."

Study identification confirmed in-text: "3. Study 1: Google Searches for 'Diet'" (Google Trends daily search-volume data, Jan 2004–Jun 2012); "4. Study 2: Undergraduate Gym [attendance]" (historical daily gym-attendance records, 11,912 gym members, 442 days); Study 3 uses "the goal-setting website" stickK.com (goal-commitment-contract records).

Landmark list confirmed verbatim (p.2, §2.1): "people are more likely to pursue various types of aspirational behavior... at the start of 'new epochs' initiated by the incidence of temporal landmarks, including the beginning of a new week, month, year, and school semester, as well as immediately following a public holiday, a school break, or a birthday."

Mechanism confirmed verbatim (p.2, §2.2, "Temporal Landmarks Relegate Past Imperfections to a Previous Mental Accounting Period"): landmarks "demarcate the passage of time and open new mental accounting periods," discontinuities that "make people feel disconnected from their past imperfections."

All elements of the claim — three studies, exact data sources, the landmark list, and the mental-accounting mechanism — match the primary text closely.

## Design facts

| design | 3 archival field studies (natural quasi-experiments on pre-existing behavioral records), regression analysis with calendar-landmark indicators and controls |
| n | Study 1: daily Google Trends search-volume series, Jan 2004–Jun 2012; Study 2: 11,912 undergraduate gym members, 442 days of attendance, gym-member fixed effects; Study 3: stickK.com goal-commitment contract records (n not read past this point) |
| population | Study 1: general public (Google searchers); Study 2: US undergraduates; Study 3: stickK users (self-selected goal-setters, likely broader adult population) — not CS-adjacent or professional-skill-specific |
| instrument | archival behavioral logs (search volume, gym check-ins, contract-creation timestamps) — not self-report |
| delay-to-test | not applicable — behavior measured continuously; effect is on likelihood of initiating aspirational behavior in the days immediately following a landmark, not a delayed retention test |
