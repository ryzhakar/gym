# N1-r4-04 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
Same analysis: small classes couldn't reach dependable measurement even with a longer instrument; a usable floor needed ~dozen-plus raters regardless of item count.

## Primary text (quote, location)
DOI 10.3389/feduc.2026.1814494, full text PDF.

Abstract: "In small classes, dependable course appraisal was difficult to attain even with longer instruments, while acceptable dependability for teaching evaluation required approximately 13–25 student raters." (pdftotext renders the en dash as "1325" — confirmed against the PDF body, not a typo in the source.)

Body, course appraisal: "Even with substantial increases in the number of evaluation items (up to 30), dependability remained low when rater counts were limited. In particular, classes with fewer than 20 students produced dependability indices that fell below commonly accepted thresholds… insufficient rater numbers restrict the stabilisation of scores, regardless of instrument length."

Body, teaching evaluation: "While dependability was low when fewer than 10 raters were available, acceptable levels of dependability were achieved when 13–25 students provided ratings, even with a relatively short instrument of 12 items."

## Design facts
Same study as N1-r4-03. "Small class" = enrolment <25 students (classification threshold stated at line ~224 of extracted text).

## Grade (S R O, changed from survey? why)
S=3, R=1, O=0 — unchanged. Note: claim compresses two distinct subscales (course appraisal never reaches dependability in small classes regardless of item count tested up to 30; teaching evaluation does reach a floor at ~13–25 raters) into one semicolon-joined sentence — this mirrors the paper's own abstract construction and is not a distortion, but a reader should not take "a usable floor" to apply to course appraisal, only to teaching evaluation.
