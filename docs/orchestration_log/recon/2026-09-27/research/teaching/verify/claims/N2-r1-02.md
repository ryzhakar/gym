# N2-r1-02 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
Interleaved practice (mixed problem types, no two consecutive problems the same) beat blocked practice on an unannounced test one month later in real 7th-grade classrooms: 61% vs 38% correct, d=0.83.

## Primary text (quote, location)
Full text read: Rohrer, Dedrick, Hartwig & Cheung (2020), "A Randomized Controlled Trial of Interleaved Mathematics Practice," *Journal of Educational Psychology* 112(1), 40–52. DOI 10.1037/edu0000367. PDF: Unpaywall returned only the publisher landing page (no `url_for_pdf`); Rohrer's current author pages (drohrer.myweb.usf.edu — connection refused; old coedu.usf.edu path — redirects to a generic USF page, dead) no longer serve it. Retrieved via Wayback Machine's archived copy of his prior author-page mirror: http://web.archive.org/web/20251227163632/http://uweb.cas.usf.edu/~drohrer/pdfs/Rohrer_et_al_2020JEdPsych.pdf — confirmed by header/page match against Crossref metadata (Vol. 112, No. 1, 40–52, same DOI printed on p.40).

Abstract (p.40):
> "Each of 54 7th-grade mathematics classes periodically completed interleaved or blocked assignments over a period of 4 months, and then both groups completed an interleaved review assignment. One month later, students took an unannounced test, and the interleaved group outscored the blocked group, 61% versus 38%, d = 0.83."

Results section (p.47–48):
> "On the test, the interleaved group outscored the blocked group by a large margin. Table 6 lists descriptive measures. The effect size was large, Cohen's d = 0.83, 95% CI = [0.68, 0.97]... We observed a positive effect for each of the 15 teachers, ds = 0.23–1.48."

Quote matches claim exactly, with the added precision that the effect held in the same direction for every one of the 15 participating teachers.

## Design facts (table)

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Design | Preregistered, cluster-randomized controlled trial (randomization within teacher: each of 15 teachers taught at least one interleaved and one blocked class) |
| n | 787 students analyzed (398 interleaved, 389 blocked) of 1,103 who began; 54 classes, 15 teachers, one US public school district |
| Population | 7th-grade mathematics students, demographically diverse (53% female; White 62%, Hispanic 19%, Black 8%, Asian 6%) |
| Instrument | 16-item unannounced test scored by 4 authors + 2 research assistants (Cronbach's alpha = .89); worksheet compliance tracked across 9 practice assignments |
| Delay-to-test | Practice ran over ~4 months; test given one month after the final (interleaved) review assignment |
