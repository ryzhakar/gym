# N5-r4-03 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed

CR usefulness decreased as the number of repeated review pairings (same reviewer/author) increased; the authors attribute this to reviewers overlooking an author's areas of strength once overly familiar, and recommend rotating reviewers: "such associations may decrease CR usefulness...we recommend rotating reviewers."

## Primary text (quote, location)

Turzo & Bosu (arXiv:2302.11686v2). The quote is spliced from two locations, not one:

- "such associations may decrease CR usefulness" — **Abstract/Conclusion** (p.1): "We recommend discouraging frequent code review associations between two individuals as such associations may decrease CR usefulness."
- "we recommend rotating reviewers" — **Discussion, §7.3 "Reviewer selection"** (p.18–19): "...the results of this study discourage 'Quid pro-Quo' reviews and recommend rotating reviewers, if possible. Moreover, such rotations will also help knowledge decentralization."

Mechanism, confirmed verbatim, same Discussion section (p.18–19): "With a higher number of mutual reviews, two persons are more likely to be aware of each others' strengths and weaknesses and therefore overlook the author's areas of strength."

Decreasing-usefulness-with-repetition finding, confirmed (p.18): "The results of our study suggest that CR usefulness decreases if the number of mutual reviews increases."

## Design facts

Same study/sample as N5-r4-01 (n=238 respondents, 2,500 labeled comments, Table 7 LR model). Predictor: MR (mutual reviews between author and reviewer), Table 7 β=-4.02e-02, 95% CI [-7.64e-02, -4.03e-03], χ²=4.745, p=0.0295* (significant negative association).
