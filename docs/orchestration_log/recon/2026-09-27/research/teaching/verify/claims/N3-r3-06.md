# N3-r3-06 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed

A systematic review of 19 purposively designed studies (8 RCTs, 5 quasi-experiments, 6 observational evaluations) on the Hawthorne effect found most studies reported some evidence that being observed/studied changes behavior, but the studies were so heterogeneous that the conditions under which the effect operates, its mechanisms, and its magnitude remain largely unknown after 60 years of the construct's use.

## Primary text (quote, location)

Full text obtained: McCambridge, J., Witton, J., Elbourne, D.R. (2014). "Systematic review of the Hawthorne effect: New concepts are needed to study research participation effects." Journal of Clinical Epidemiology 67(3), 267-277. Full-text XML via Europe PMC (`https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC3969247/fullTextXML`).

Abstract: "Nineteen purposively designed studies were included, providing quantitative data on the size of the effect in eight randomized controlled trials, five quasiexperimental studies, and six observational evaluations of reporting on one's behavior... Although all but one study was undertaken within health sciences, study methods, contexts, and findings were highly heterogeneous. Most studies reported some evidence of an effect, although significant biases are judged likely... Consequences of research participation for behaviors being investigated do exist, although little can be securely known about the conditions under which they operate, their mechanisms of effects, or their magnitudes."

"What is new?" box (§1 Introduction): "Most of the 19 purposively designed evaluation studies included in this systematic review provide some evidence of research participation effects. The heterogeneity of these studies means that little can be confidently inferred about the size of these effects, the conditions under which they operate, or their mechanisms. ... As the Hawthorne effect construct has not successfully led to important research advances in this area over a period of 60 years, new concepts are needed to guide empirical studies."

Both quotes match the claim and the claims.csv-cited text verbatim.
