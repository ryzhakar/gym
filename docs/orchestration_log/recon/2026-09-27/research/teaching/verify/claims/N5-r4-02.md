# N5-r4-02 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed

The same study found no significant association between CR-comment usefulness and a reviewer's prior authorship/reviewership experience with the specific file under review -- directly contradicting Bosu et al. (2015)'s Microsoft-based finding that file-specific familiarity predicts useful reviews: "contrasting their findings we do not find any significant association."

## Primary text (quote, location)

Turzo & Bosu (arXiv:2302.11686v2, 24pp), Discussion §7.

The exact quoted sentence is at p.18, item 3 ("Comparison with other studies investigating CR usefulness") — but it is about **Rahman et al.**, not Bosu et al.:

> "Rahman et al. investigated the relationship between developer experience and CR usefulness. Our results concur with their findings that code-commit experience has positive associations. However, contrasting their findings we do not find any significant association between CR usefulness and authorship/reviewership for the file under review."

The Bosu-et-al.-specific contradiction is stated separately, in different wording, at p.18, item 1 ("Comparison with Bosu et al."):

> "Bosu et al. found that a reviewer's prior experience with the file under review, either as a reviewer or as an author, positively associates with the likelihood of providing useful reviews. However, we found none of the factors to measure these experiences (i.e., RCS, log PFRC, and log COMC) having significant associations with usefulness scores in our study."

**Finding: quote misattributed.** The literal sentence in claims.csv belongs to the Rahman-et-al. paragraph, not Bosu et al. The substantive claim (no significant association with file-specific reviewer/author experience, contradicting Bosu et al. 2015's Microsoft finding) is nonetheless independently and correctly supported, in the adjacent Bosu-et-al. bullet, using non-matching wording. Both Bosu et al. (2015, Microsoft) and Rahman et al. are prior studies this paper contrasts itself against on this exact point.

## Design facts

Same study/sample as N5-r4-01 (n=238 survey respondents, 2,500 labeled comments, Table 7 LR model). Table 7 coefficients for the three file-familiarity proxies named in the Bosu-et-al. bullet: RCS β=-6.37e-03, p=0.5488 (n.s.); log PFRC β=1.69e-02, p=0.6301 (n.s.); log COMC was dropped pre-modeling for multicollinearity (clustered with RCS/WRC/RCSH, |ρ|≥0.7 — RCS retained as cluster representative) but is named directly in the discussion's non-significance claim.
