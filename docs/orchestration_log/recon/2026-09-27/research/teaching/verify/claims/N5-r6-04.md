# N5-r6-04 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
"In a 3-arm RCT, AI-generated formative feedback (VOA) produced significantly larger objective skill-metric gains during practice than a live remote expert surgical instructor did (0.66-point Expertise Score advantage over the instructor arm, P<.001); the human expert instructor's practice-phase gain over no-feedback control was not significant."
Source: 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2021.49008 (Fazlollahi et al. — "Effect of Artificial Intelligence Tutoring vs Expert Instruction on Learning Simulated Surgical Skills Among Medical Students: A Randomized Clinical Trial")

## Primary text (quote, location)
Results: "A total of 70 medical students... were randomized, including 23 students in the VOA group, 24 students in the instructor group, and 23 students in the control group... VOA significantly improved practice Expertise Scores by 0.66 (95% CI, 0.55 to 0.77) points compared with the instructor group and by 0.65 (95% CI, 0.54 to 0.77) points compared with the control group (P < .001)... Mean Expertise Scores in instructor and control groups were not significantly different." (Results, PMC full text, ~line 65 and ~line 130 of cached text)
full_text_read=Y (PMC8864513, open access).

## Design facts (table)
| field | value |
|---|---|
| design | 3-arm RCT: AI tutor (VOA) vs. expert remote instructor vs. no-feedback control; block-randomized 1:1:1 |
| population | 70 medical students (41 women, 29 men; mean age 21.8), 4 institutions |
| instrument | ICEMS Expertise Score (algorithmic, per-practice-resection) + blinded OSATS on a final "realistic" resection |
| delay-to-test | none — 5 practice resections with feedback, then 1 realistic resection in the same session ("learning and transfer of technical skills" evaluated immediately, not after a retention interval) |
