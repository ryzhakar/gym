# N3-r5-02 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
A meta-analysis pooling 65 independent evaluations of school tutoring programs found tutored students outperformed non-tutored control students on examinations and developed more positive attitudes toward the tutored subject; tutors themselves also gained subject understanding. DOI 10.3102/00028312019002237 (Cohen, J.A. Kulik & C-L.C. Kulik, "Educational Outcomes of Tutoring: A Meta-analysis of Findings," AERJ 1982).

## Primary text (quote, location)
DOI full text unreachable: Unpaywall `is_oa:false`, no arXiv/Semantic Scholar/EuropePMC/OpenAlex/CORE hit, browser fetch hit a bot-wall. Crossref abstract: "A meta-analysis of findings from 65 independent evaluations of school tutoring programs showed that these programs have positive effects on the academic performance and attitudes of those who receive tutoring. Tutored students outperformed control students on examinations, and they also developed positive attitudes toward the subject matter... tutoring programs have positive effects on children who serve as tutors... gained a better understanding of and developed more positive attitudes toward the subject matter."

Fetched by hand: ERIC ED204416, Cohen (sole author), "Educational Outcomes of Tutoring: A Research Synthesis," AERA conference paper, April 1981 — the same 65-study corpus and lead author, one year before the AERJ journal publication. Quotes from that text (p.3-8 of the PDF):
- "A total of 65 of the 250 reports contained data that could [be used]..." (p.3)
- "Fifty-two of the 65 studies examined the effect [of tutoring on achievement]... outperformed pupils who were not tutored in 87% of these studies... the [average effect size] the 52 studies was .40" (p.4)
- "In all eight studies [that] reported data on tutee attitude... class in 87% of the 38 studies [tutor attitude]... average [effect size] in the 38 studies was .33" (p.5)
- "Self-concept effects. Nine studies reported data on tutee self-concept... average effect size for the nine studies was only .09" (p.4)

## Design facts
Meta-analysis, k=65 evaluations (52 on achievement, 38 on tutor attitude, 9 on tutee self-concept, 11-16 on other subscales). Population: school tutoring programs (K-12, per the ERIC descriptors: elementary/secondary). Outcome: exam/achievement scores and Likert-type attitude measures, no stated delay-to-test (immediate post-program comparison implied).

## Replications & critics

Broader search found later, larger updates of the same question: Nickow, Oreopoulos & Quan, "The Impressive Effects of Tutoring on PreK-12 Learning: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of the Experimental Evidence" (NBER w27476, 2020) and Xu et al., "Peer Tutoring and Academic Achievement in Mathematics: A Meta-Analysis" (10.12973/ejmste/79805, 2017) — both supersede rather than refute Cohen 1982's direction of effect.
