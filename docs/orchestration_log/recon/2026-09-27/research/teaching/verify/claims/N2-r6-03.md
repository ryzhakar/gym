# N2-r6-03 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
Combining a fading worked-example-to-problem schedule with self-explanation prompts (naming the underlying principle at each fading step) produced medium-to-large effects on BOTH near- and far-transfer performance across two experiments — extending fading's previously near-transfer-only benefit to far transfer as well. DOI 10.1037/0022-0663.95.4.774 (Atkinson, Renkl & Merrill, 2003).

## Primary text (quote, location)
Full text is closed access (APA PsycArticles paywall; Unpaywall confirms `oa_status: closed`). Only the APA PsycNet structured abstract was read:

> "Although research has demonstrated that successively fading or successively removing more and more worked-out solution steps as learners transition from relying on examples to independent problem solving reliably fosters performance on near-transfer tasks — relative to example-problem pair — this effect is not reliable on far-transfer tasks. To address this, the authors combined fading with the introduction of prompts designed to encourage learners to identify the underlying principle illustrated in each worked-out solution step. Across 2 experiments, this combination produced medium to large effects on near and far transfer without requiring additional time on task."

Every element of the claim (fading + principle-naming self-explanation prompts; 2 experiments; medium-to-large effects on both near AND far transfer; extending fading's previously near-transfer-only benefit) matches this abstract verbatim.
