# N2-r4-07 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
A causal-structure-learning method recovered the prerequisite relationships among 8 skills from 535 students' real binary right/wrong responses on 20 fraction-subtraction exercises substantially more accurately (84% of true adjacencies recovered) than four prior baseline structure-discovery methods on the same real-world dataset.

## Primary text (quote, location)
Full text read: Cai, ... et al., "Causal-Driven Skill Prerequisite Structure Discovery," *Proceedings of the AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence* 38(18), 2024. DOI 10.1609/aaai.v38i18.30046. PDF: https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/30046/31841 (Unpaywall OA location, downloaded directly, `pdftotext -layout`).

"Real-World Test Data" section (p. 20608–20609):
> "We compare CSPS with the baseline approaches in Fig. 5. Considering the adjacency score first (Fig. 5a), CSPS shows the best performance in discovering the adjacencies (see F1-AR). The value of TPAR achieves 0.84, which means that we recover 84% adjacencies for FrcSub. Note that EPS-ND and PARM perform well in terms of TPAR, however, they pay a price for the highest true positive adjacency rate because their values of TDAR are lower, which means that EPS-ND and PARM tend to allow for a lot of redundant edges, blurring the true prerequisite structure."

"Baseline Approaches" section (p. 20608), enumerating every method compared against CSPS on FrcSub (Fig. 5's legend lists all six by name — EPS-ND, CITS, PARM, CMPD, BE-NMC, DIDACT — alongside CSPS):
> "• EPS-ND (Brunskill 2011) ... • CITS (Scheines, Silver, and Goldin 2014) ... • PARM (Chen, Wuillemin, and Labat 2015) ... • CMPD (Chen, González-Brenes, and Tian 2016) ... • BE-NMC (Han, Yoon, and Yoo 2017) ... • DIDACT (Saarinen, Cater, and Littman 2020) ..."

The claim's own numeric quote (TPAR=0.84 / "84%") is accurate as a transcription, but it materially mischaracterizes what the paper reports:

1. **Baseline count is wrong.** The paper compares CSPS against six named baselines on FrcSub (Fig. 5), not four: EPS-ND, CITS, PARM, CMPD, BE-NMC, DIDACT.
2. **The "more accurate" claim inverts the paper's own caveat.** TPAR (0.84) is CSPS's *adjacency recall* rate, not its overall accuracy. The very next sentence states EPS-ND and PARM have TPAR *at least as high or higher* than CSPS's 0.84 ("EPS-ND and PARM perform well in terms of TPAR") — they are penalized on TDAR (a precision-like measure) instead. CSPS's actual superiority claimed by the authors is on the combined **F1-AR** metric (precision+recall balanced), not on raw adjacency recall. "84% of true adjacencies recovered... more accurately than [baselines]" reads as a recall claim, and on recall specifically, CSPS is not the leader.
