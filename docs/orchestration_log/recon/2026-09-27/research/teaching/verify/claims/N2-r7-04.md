# N2-r7-04 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
Spacing/sequencing changes retention yield even when total practice time on the item is held equal; the effect is about order, not more hours.

## Primary text (quote, location)
DOI 10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354 — same source as N2-r7-03 (Cepeda et al., 2006). Full text already cached; re-used here.

General Discussion (p.365-366): "prior meta-analyses (Donovan & Radosevich, 1999; Janiszewski et al., 2003; T. D. Lee & Genovese, 1988) failed to distinguish spacing effects (a single presentation, or a lag less than 1 s, vs. multiple presentations, or a lag of 1 s or more, of a given item; equal total study time for that item, whether in the spaced or massed condition) from lag effects (less vs. more time between..."

This sentence is the paper's own explicit definition of "the spacing effect": a comparison between massed and spaced presentation *with total study time for the item held equal* — directly confirming the claim's core point that the effect is about ordering/timing of exposures, not additional total practice time.

**Location correction.** The survey cites "p.365, Table 5 section (Difference lag analyses)." The quote above is in the General Discussion text on p.365-366, not inside Table 5 itself — Table 5 in this paper (p.366) is titled "Shorter and Longer Interstudy Interval (ISI) Range... for Absolute Lag Analyses," not "Difference lag analyses." The substantive claim is fully supported by the General Discussion text; the specific table/section pointer in the survey's citation is imprecise and should be corrected to "General Discussion, p.365-366" rather than "Table 5, Difference lag analyses."
