# N2-r5-01 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
Across 34 studies / 1,421 participants, high-contextual-interference (randomly ordered / interleaved) practice produced a medium overall benefit on delayed transfer versus low-CI (blocked) practice. DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1377122 (Czyż, Wójcik & Solarská, "The effect of contextual interference on transfer in motor learning," Frontiers in Psychology, 2024).

## Primary text (quote, location)
Full text (Frontiers OA HTML→text, cached). Abstract/p.1: "The overall CI effect on transfer in motor learning was medium (SMD = 0.55), favoring random practice." Results, "Meta-analysis: results" (p.7): "The analysis of the CI effect on delayed transfer (Figure 2) covered 34 studies, yielding 86 effect sizes and resulting in testing 1,421 participants. The pooled effect size based on the three-level meta-analytic model was medium SMD = 0.55 (95% CI: 0.25, 0.86; p < 0.001)."

## Design facts
Systematic review + 3-level mixed-effects meta-analysis, PROSPERO-registered (CRD42021228267), PRISMA-guided, 42 studies in the systematic review / 34 in the quantitative synthesis, 86 effect sizes, n=1,421 across studies. Population: healthy participants, ages 5.5–82, various motor tasks (sport skills, laparoscopic surgery, handwriting, balance). Outcome: delayed transfer tests only (immediate-transfer studies excluded from the meta-analysis by design). Sensitivity analysis (Cook's D) noted: pooled SMD drops to 0.40 once 5 outlier outcomes are removed.

## Retraction check
Crossref `works/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1377122`: no `update-to`, no `relation`. Not retracted.

## Replications & critics
OpenAlex `cites:W4401587839` (8 total, all fetched): "Advancing Contextual Interference: Addressing Methodological Debates, Reflecting on Meta-Analytic Practices and Generalizability, and Guiding Future Directions in Motor Learning" (10.1007/s10648-025-10043-1) is a direct methodological follow-up/critique of this meta-analytic literature. Within its own text, the paper reports its result diverges from a prior meta-analysis, Ammar et al. 2023 ("the myth of contextual interference learning benefit in sports practice," 10.1016/J.EDUREV.2023.100537), which found a smaller, non-significant effect (SMD=0.243) — a direct point of disagreement in the literature, both sides represented.
