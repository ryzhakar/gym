## Claim as surveyed
"In a 4-week multisite RCT of an AI tutoring platform against business-as-usual self-directed revision, students allocated to the platform scored higher on curriculum-aligned post-tests (Hedges' g=0.33, 95% CI 0.18-0.48), with positive effects across all three science subjects tested and no differential impact by disadvantage status; attrition was substantial (30.7%) and the authors call the findings preliminary." ## Primary text (quote, location)
Full text fetched (`arxiv.org/pdf/2609.14789v1`, 12 pp.).

Abstract (p.1): "a four-week multisite individually randomised evaluation of Medly... in GCSE Biology, Chemistry and Physics... Of 929 students completing baseline assessment, 644 completed post-testing... students allocated to Medly achieved higher post-test attainment than students undertaking business-as-usual self-directed revision (Hedges' g = 0.33, 95% CI 0.18 to 0.48). Positive estimates were observed in Physics (g = 0.31), Chemistry (g = 0.32) and Biology (g = 0.52), with no evidence of differential impact by disadvantage status... Attrition was substantial (30.7%)... we interpret the findings as preliminary."

Methods (p.~4): "Post- and pre-tests were designed... assessment was not a standardised instrument; responses were AI-marked and then manually checked" and "students completed a 30-minute baseline assessment" — pre/post assessment is separate from platform use, administered in the normal classroom setting by participating teachers ("the participating teachers entered the pre- and post-test data," p.~9), no AI access implied during testing itself.

Results (p.6): "Total 929 644 30.68%" (attrition table); "corresponding to Hedges' g = 0.33 (95% CI 0.18 to 0.48)."

All numbers in the claim match the primary text exactly.
