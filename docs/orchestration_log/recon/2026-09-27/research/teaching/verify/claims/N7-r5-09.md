## Primary text (quote, location)
Same full PDF as N7-r5-03 (Sabrina & Anggelia, ICoBITS Vol. 1). Abstract and Section 3.2 ("Learner Engagement, Motivation, and Perceptions"):

Abstract: "The human tutor group achieved slightly higher gains in sentence construction, indicating deeper conceptual understanding, while the chatbot group reported higher engagement, reduced anxiety, and greater learner autonomy."

Section 3.2, interview quotes (translated from Indonesian): "The good thing about chatbots is that I'm not afraid of making mistakes. I can try again immediately without feeling judged" (A); "With chatbots, I'm not worried about missing explanations because I can ask anytime without hesitation" (RS). Exact match to the claim.

## Design facts
Same trial as N7-r5-03 — see that file (quasi-experimental, n=30, 2 non-randomized groups, EFL undergraduates, single session, pre/post grammar test plus semi-structured interviews analyzed thematically for the engagement/anxiety/autonomy findings this claim covers).

Additional instrument detail for this claim specifically: engagement/anxiety/autonomy are qualitative, drawn from semi-structured post-hoc interviews with inductive thematic coding — self-report, not a validated scale.
