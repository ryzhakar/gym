# N6-r5-09 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
In a survey of 327 contributors across 12 Free/Libre/Open-Source-Software communities, task performance was directly predicted by how purposeful contributors found their assigned tasks, and by interaction intensity and community supportiveness acting through contributors' sense of social identification with the community — in this self-directed, community-based CS-adjacent population, what sustains contribution and performance is substantially social (mentoring, purposeful task assignment, identification), not scheduling or individual attention traits. DOI 10.26686/wgtn.17007352.

In Model 2, the same result was found with task purposefulness (path coefficient of 0.244 with p<0.001) while social integration was also found to positively influence task performance (0.348, p<0.001)."

**Correction from the surveyed claim, found on the full-text read:** the full text (Ch.7 summary, p.207) states the interaction-intensity/supportiveness→task-performance link is fully mediated by **both** social identification **and** social integration — not by social identification alone, as the claim (following the abstract's shorter phrasing) states. The abstract itself compresses this to "social identification," so the claim's wording traces to the paper's own abstract, but the more precise finding in the body is two mediators, not one. Separately, the full text (Ch.8.6.1, Model 1) also names joining structuredness as a third direct predictor of task performance alongside task purposefulness and supportiveness — an element the claim omits.
