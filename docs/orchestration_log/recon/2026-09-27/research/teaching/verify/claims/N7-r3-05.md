## Claim as surveyed
"Pooled AI tutoring (13 different AI tutors) produced learning gains on unaided GRE questions, after one hour of tutoring, statistically equivalent to expert human tutoring (p=.015), with the best-performing AI tutor beating the human tutor in 5 of 7 GRE domains; in a second comparison, one AI tutor's generated lesson plans and practice problems were rated by expert human tutors as producing equivalent learning gains (p=.044) at roughly 918x lower cost." ## DISCREPANCY — misattribution of the 918x/p=.044 result
The claim's second clause states this figure comes from "a second comparison" in which "one AI tutor's generated lesson plans and practice problems were rated by expert human tutors as producing equivalent learning gains." This is not what the source reports. The p=.044/918× figure belongs to Study 1 (the live-tutoring learning-gains equivalence test), specifically to Gemma 4 31B's individual equivalence test against the human tutor's measured learning gains — not to the separate lesson-plan/practice-problem rubric-rating study (Study 2), which the paper explicitly states measures teaching quality by expert preference, "not based on how much learning occurs." The claim conflates two distinct sub-studies of the same paper.
