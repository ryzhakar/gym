# N1-r3-12 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
A confirmatory-factor-analysis validation (n=1,960 adult distance learners) produced a psychometrically sound self-directed-learning diagnostic instrument, but validity here means internal factor fit, not any demonstrated link to actually-sustained independent study behavior.

## Primary text (quote, location)
Khiat, H. (2015). "Measuring Self-Directed Learning: A Diagnostic Tool for Adult Learners." Journal of University Teaching and Learning Practice, 12(2), Art. 2. DOI 10.53761/1.12.2.2. Full text read (repository PDF, University of Wollongong Research Online).

Abstract, p.1: "1,960 students responded to the survey, and the statistical methods of exploratory and confirmatory factor analysis were used in the analysis. The confirmatory ... model ... has a good fit on the data, thus affirming the strong validity and reliability of the tool."

Methodology, p.24: "an invitation to participate in an anonymous and voluntary online survey was sent to 12,698 students... A total of 1,695 students responded to the survey (a 13.4% response rate), and 1,219 students completed it... The respondents were randomly selected into two groups... One group consisted of 741 respondents... used in the exploratory factor analysis... The other group consisted of 478 respondents whose data were used in the confirmatory factor analysis process."

## Design facts

Report design (Tables 4-6) shows descriptive mean SDL-domain scores split by self-reported CGPA band (e.g., CGPA 0-1.99 subgroup, n=42) as reference points for a feedback report — no correlation, regression, or significance test relates SDL score to CGPA or any other sustained-study-behavior measure. |

## Grade (S R O, changed from survey? why)
S=3, R=2 — unchanged. O=0 confirmed and strengthened: the primary text shows no statistical test at all linking the instrument to behavior, only descriptive CGPA-band means built for a feedback report, not a validation analysis.

Changed from survey: the claim's stated n (1,960) is wrong for what it is attached to. The paper's own abstract (1,960) disagrees with its own methods section (1,695 responded / 1,219 completed), and neither figure is the sample the CFA ran on — the CFA specifically used n=478, a subset held out from the 741 used for the EFA. A claim reporting "CFA validation (n=1,960...)" overstates the CFA's actual sample size by roughly 4x.

## Status: REFUTED
Qualitative substance (good CFA fit; no demonstrated behavioral validity) holds and is confirmed by primary text — REFUTED applies specifically to the n=1,960 figure misattributed to the CFA.
