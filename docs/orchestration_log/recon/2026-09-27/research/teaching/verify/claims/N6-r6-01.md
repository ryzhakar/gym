# N6-r6-01 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
Once-weekly VR surgical-skill training produced worse initial skill acquisition than daily training, but the weekly group's error count plateaued near baseline through 3 months while the daily group's errors climbed steadily from 1 week to 3 months post-training — spaced practice traded acquisition speed for durable retention. DOI 10.1302/1358-992x.2023.7.104.

## Primary text (quote, location)
Full text is not reachable: this is a conference-proceedings abstract record (Bone & Joint Open/Orthopaedic Proceedings); `cache.py` returned only the abstract (route `abstract`). The abstract itself, however, is fully quantitative:

> "The daily group demonstrated faster skills acquisition, reducing the median ± IQR number of procedural errors from 68 ± 67.05 (session one) to 7 ± 9.75 (session four), compared to the weekly group's improvement from 63 ± 27 (session one) to 13 ± 15.75 (session four), p < 0.001. The weekly group error count plateaued remaining at 14 ± 6.75 at one-week, 16.50 ± 16.25 at one-month and 26.45 ± 22 at 3-months, p < 0.05. However, the daily group showed poorer retention with error counts rising to 16 ± 12.25 at one-week, 17.50 ± 23 at one-month and 41.45 ± 26 at 3-months, p<0.01."

**Precision note:** "plateaued near baseline" is a loose characterization. The weekly group's error count at 3 months (26.45) is roughly double its session-four low point (13), not flat at that low point — it does rise, just far less steeply than the daily group's rise (13 → 41.45, roughly 3×). "Plateaued" is defensible only relative to the daily group's much steeper climb, not in an absolute sense against the weekly group's own post-training minimum.
