# N6-r6-02 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
Daily (massed/burst-style) practice reduced procedural errors faster across four training sessions than once-weekly (spaced) practice on the identical simulated skill, a direct skill-per-hour advantage for burst scheduling that reversed once training stopped. DOI 10.1302/1358-992x.2023.7.104.

## Primary text (quote, location)
Same source and same access limitation as N6-r6-01 (abstract-only conference-proceedings record):

> "The daily group demonstrated faster skills acquisition, reducing the median ± IQR number of procedural errors from 68 ± 67.05 (session one) to 7 ± 9.75 (session four), compared to the weekly group's improvement from 63 ± 27 (session one) to 13 ± 15.75 (session four), p < 0.001."

And, on the reversal after training stopped (same abstract, quoted in full at N6-r6-01): the daily group's errors rose from 7±9.75 to 41.45±26 by 3 months, while the weekly group's rose from 13±15.75 to only 26.45±22 — i.e., the daily group's lower training-end error count is overtaken/reversed in relative retention terms. Both halves of the claim (faster reduction during training; reversal after training stopped) match the abstract's numbers.
