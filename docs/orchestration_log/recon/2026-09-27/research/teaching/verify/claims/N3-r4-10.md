# N3-r4-10 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
"Incorporating students' hint-usage history into a proactive help-need predictor reduced false negatives and improved prediction of when help was actually needed, versus a predictor that ignored hint usage -- an evidence base for calibrating how much struggle to allow before intervening." (arXiv:2207.03025, quote from Abstract)

## Primary text (quote, location)
Full text fetched and read. Abstract:

> "We show that incorporating students' hint usage significantly improves the adaptive hint policy's efficacy in predicting students' HelpNeed, thereby reducing training unproductivity, reducing possible help avoidance, and increasing possible help appropriateness (a higher chance of receiving help when it was likely to be needed)."

Confirmed in Results §6.2.1 (lines 862–880), which reports the specific false-negative comparison the claim describes:

> "Figure 1 shows that, on average, the HNU predictor has 7.1% instances with a false negative (SD = 4.2), and the HN predictor has 11.5% false negatives (SD = 9.2). A Mann Whitney test shows a significant difference (U = 768.5, z = 2.93, p = .002) between the two predictors in the percentage of false negatives, with the HNU predictor performing significantly better than the HN predictor. This confirms our H2 hypothesis."

This upgrades the claim from an abstract-level paraphrase to a body-confirmed statistic: 7.1% vs. 11.5% false negatives, p=.002 (Mann-Whitney U=768.5, z=2.93), on the same HNU Adaptive condition's data with two competing predictors (controlling for extraneous factors, per the text's own methodological note).

## Design facts (table)
Same study as N3-r4-08/09: n=74 analyzed of 84 randomized, undergraduate discrete-math students. This specific comparison is a within-condition predictor-vs-predictor analysis (HNU predictor vs. HN predictor scored on the same HNU-Adaptive-condition data), not a between-subjects outcome measure — no delay-to-test applies here; it is a modeling/prediction-accuracy result, not a learning outcome.
