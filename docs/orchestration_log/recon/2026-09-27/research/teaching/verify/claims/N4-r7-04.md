# N4-r7-04 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
"When teachers (pre-service and in-service, secondary level) judge a learner's self-regulated-learning skill from work samples, the presence of non-diagnostic cues (handwriting quality, an unrelated social-behavior report) significantly reduces judgment accuracy relative to diagnostic cues alone (global judgment F(2,261)=14.01, p<.001, small-to-medium eta-squared=0.09), and explicitly telling teachers which cues are non-diagnostic does not restore their accuracy (F(1,261)=1.56, p=.21)." (DOI 10.1007/s11409-026-09455-3)

## Primary text (quote, location)
Full text fetched (Springer, open access, *Metacognition and Learning* 2026; Tannert, Karlen, Ader, Hertel & Glogger-Frey).

Results, H1.1: "For the global rating, the ANOVA showed a significant difference between the experimental groups, F (2, 261) = 14.01, p <.001, ηp² = 0.09, which represents a recommended minimum effect size indicating practical relevance of the effect (RMPE)."

Results, H2.1: "The critical contrast between the informed (iDND) and the non-informed condition (DND) was not significant, neither for the global judgment accuracy, F (1, 261) = 1.56, p =.21, ηp² = 0.01, nor the single judgment accuracy, F (1, 261) = 2.71, p =.101... Thus, information on the low diagnostic value of the handwriting and the social school report had no positive effect on the judgment accuracy."

Exact numeric match on both F-tests.

## Design facts

| field | value |
|---|---|
| design | preregistered (OSF), fully randomized 3-condition experiment (D / DND / iDND), multi-lab (Switzerland/Turkey/Germany) |
| n | 262–263 (pre-service and in-service) secondary-school teachers (D n=83, DND n=84, iDND n=95–96) |
| population | pre-service and in-service secondary teachers judging fictive students' self-regulated-learning (SRL) skill from learning-journal work samples |
| instrument | global (1–7 scale) and single-strategy (0/1/cannot-judge) judgment-accuracy scores against expert ratings, on incongruent-cue student materials |
| delay-to-test | none — single online session, judgments made immediately after viewing materials |

## Replications & critics
Crossref `is-referenced-by-count: 4` (brand-new, April 2026). The paper itself directly engages and partially contradicts a prior study (van De Pol et al. 2024) on the informed-condition manipulation: "Inconsistent with findings by van De Pol et al. (2024), participants who were informed about the low diagnostic value of certain cues demonstrated an equally low global judgment accuracy as those who were not informed." Not independently read this round.
