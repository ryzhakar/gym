# N4-r2-05 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
In a 7-country, 556-student study, CS1 students scoring in the middle 50% on 12 tracing/code-completion MCQs demonstrated conceptual grasp of the tested constructs (they answered some items correctly) but failed specifically at executing the multi-step chain of reasoning tracing requires -- correctly diagnosing this population's gap requires distinguishing a reasoning-chain-execution deficit from a conceptual-understanding deficit, which a single pass/fail score cannot do.

Attributed to DOI 10.1145/1041624.1041673 (Lister et al., "A Multi-National Study of Reading and Tracing Skills in Novice Programmers," ACM SIGCSE Bulletin, 2004).

## Primary text (quote, location)
Full text obtained: Unpaywall green OA copy, University of Technology Sydney repository, `https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/bitstream/10453/4126/3/2004000904.pdf` (32 pp., submitted version).

§3.9 Performance Data Discussion, p.132 (SIGCSE Bulletin pagination), p.10 of PDF:

> "One observation we make about the middle 50% of students is that, by virtue of the fact that they answered some questions correctly, they have demonstrated a conceptual grasp of loops and arrays. Therefore, the weakness of these students is not that they do not understand the language constructs. Their weakness is the inability to reliably work their way through the long chain of reasoning required to hand execute code, and/or an inability to reason reliably at a more abstract level to select the missing line of code. Based on the performance data, it is not possible to draw any firm conclusions as to the exact cause of this weakness."

§3.7 Reliability confirms N=556 ("Using the data for students who were given all 12 MCQs (N=556), we obtained a value of 0.75"). §1/Abstract confirms seven countries: "students from seven countries."

## Status: VERIFIED

Caveat: the survey claim's framing overstates certainty. The source explicitly hedges with "and/or" between two candidate sub-causes (chain-of-reasoning execution vs. abstract-level reasoning to select the missing line) and states outright: "it is not possible to draw any firm conclusions as to the exact cause of this weakness." The quoted finding (middle-quartile students show conceptual grasp but fail at reliable multi-step reasoning) is textually accurate; the claim's implication that a reasoning-chain-execution deficit is *the* established, singular cause goes beyond what the authors assert.
