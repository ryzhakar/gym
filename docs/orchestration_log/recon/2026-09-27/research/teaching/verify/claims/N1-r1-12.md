# N1-r1-12 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
"Across k=430 studies (N=90,380) on workplace and educational self-regulated learning, self-report constructs most associated with sustaining practice (goal level, persistence, effort, self-efficacy) jointly account for only 17% of the variance in learning -- a weak-to-moderate proxy for whether practice will actually be sustained." (DOI 10.1037/a0022777)

Best available primary-source text: the authors' own abstract, as deposited by APA/MEDLINE, retrieved via PubMed (PMID 21401218) — not a secondary summary:

"Meta-analytic findings (k=430, N=90,380) support theoretical propositions that self-regulation constructs are interrelated... Goal level, persistence, effort, and self-efficacy were the self-regulation constructs with the strongest effects on learning. Together these constructs accounted for 17% of the variance in learning, after controlling for cognitive ability and pretraining knowledge."

k, N, the four named constructs, and the 17% figure all match the claim exactly.

One mismatch: the abstract's outcome variable is **learning** (a training/knowledge-test outcome), not "sustaining practice." The claim's framing — "self-report constructs most associated with sustaining practice," "a proxy for whether practice will actually be sustained" — is the surveyor's interpretive bridge from "predicts learning" to "predicts sustained practice." The primary source, at the abstract level, supports the former; it does not itself claim to measure practice-continuation/sustained-behavior as an outcome. Full text (not read) would be needed to confirm whether any of the 16 constructs or dependent measures in the paper operationalize sustained practice directly.
