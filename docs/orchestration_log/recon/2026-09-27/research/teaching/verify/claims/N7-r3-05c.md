# N7-r3-05c — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
Corrected replacement for N7-r3-05's second clause: "One individual AI tutor (Gemma 4 31B) achieved GRE learning gains, on live tutored students, statistically equivalent to expert human tutoring's own measured learning gains (p=.044) at approximately 918x lower cost per percentage point of learning gain — the same live-tutoring study as the pooled-AI-vs-human equivalence result, not the separate lesson-plan/practice-problem expert-rating study." DOI 10.48550/arxiv.2609.28470.

## Why this claim exists
N7-r3-05 (see `verify/claims/N7-r3-05.md`) attributed this p=.044/918x figure to the paper's separate lesson-plan/practice-problem rubric-rating study (2,028 expert pairwise reviews). The source does not support that attribution — it explicitly states the rubric/leaderboard evaluations are "not based on how much learning occurs." The figure instead comes from Study 1's live-tutoring learning-gains data: it is the individual equivalence test for one specific low-cost AI tutor, Gemma 4 31B, against the human tutor's own measured learning gains. This entry restates the finding with correct attribution. DOI/ID resolution, retraction check and replication search were already done for N7-r3-05 and are not repeated — see that file.

## Primary text (quote, location)
Full text: `https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.28470v1`.

- §6 "The cost per percentage point of learning gain": "We test whether each AI tutor's learning gains are equivalent to human tutoring, and find that Gemma 4 31B achieved GRE learning gains equivalent to expert human tutoring (p=.044) at 918× lower cost per percentage point of learning gain."
- §10 Conclusion (restates the same figure): "Among the six AI tutors that passed individual equivalence tests, Gemma 4 31B achieved these gains at 918× lower cost per percentage point than human tutoring."
- Abstract, same figure: "one AI tutor achieved learning gains equivalent to human tutoring (p = .044) at 918 times lower cost (USD 0.0052 for AI versus USD 4.81 for human, per percentage point gained)."
- Contrast, §9 Limitations, on the separate rating study: "The leaderboard evaluations in Figure 3 characterize teaching quality based on expert tutor evaluations and observed conversational behaviors, not based on how much learning occurs." This is the explicit textual basis for ruling out the original claim's attribution to the rating study.

## Design facts, retraction check, replications
Identical underlying study (Study 1: 2,383 participants, AI/human/no-tutoring arms, TOST equivalence testing, ±0.25-SD bounds) — see N7-r3-05 for the full design table. No retraction. No replications found (paper 4 days old at check time).

## Grade (S R O)
S3, R2, O1 — unchanged from N7-r3-05's grading; same study, same population (adults, GRE test-prep domain), same immediate-only outcome measurement.

## Status: VERIFIED
The p=.044 and 918× figures, and their attribution to a single AI tutor's (Gemma 4 31B) own live-tutoring learning-gains equivalence test rather than to the lesson-plan rating study, are verbatim-confirmed in three separate locations in the primary text (Abstract, §6, §10). This replaces N7-r3-05's second clause as the citable form of this finding; N7-r3-05 stays REFUTED as a record of the original misattribution.
