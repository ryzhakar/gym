# N7-r6-11 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
Across pre-LLM intelligent tutoring and ed-tech programs generally, an evidence review of many RCTs/quasi-experiments finds effect sizes are mixed and often null outside a handful of well-run RCTs, with positive cases in the 0.18-0.29+ SD range — a baseline against which the newer LLM-tutor effect sizes above (0.63-0.73, 4-9pp, 5.5pp) can be read as comparatively large, pending independent replication. DOI 10.3386/w23744 (Escueta, Quan, Nickow & Oreopoulos, 2017, NBER Working Paper "Education Technology: An Evidence-Based Review").

## Primary text (quote, location)
Full text read (Unpaywall open-access copy). Conclusion (p.87):

> "We found that simply providing students with access to technology yields largely mixed results... From our review, computer-assisted learning and behavioral interventions emerge as two areas that show considerable promise... Two interventions in the United States stand out as being particularly promising — a fairly low-intensity online program that provides students with immediate feedback on math homework was found to have an effect size of 0.18 standard deviations, and a more intensive software-based math curriculum intervention improved seventh and eighth grade math scores by a remarkable 0.63 and 0.56 standard deviations."

Body (p.22), on the "mixed/often null outside a handful of well-run RCTs" framing:

> "Campuzano et al., 2009 and Dynarski et al., 2007 represent notable exceptions to the overall pattern of findings. These Department of Education studies evaluated roughly a dozen CAL programs [and] found a general pattern of no effects... Of those evaluated [elsewhere in the review], several interventions show especially strong promise, e.g., an evaluation of a math homework program in Maine showed an effect size of 0.18 standard deviations... while a more intensive software-based math curriculum intervention in Texas [the SimCalc study] improved seventh and eighth grade math scores by 0.63 and 0.56 standard deviations, respectively."

**Correction from the surveyed claim:** the review's own reported *positive* effect-size range (from the specific lines the survey cited, ~1278/1431/1503) runs from **0.18 up to 0.63** SD (the SimCalc study, explicitly called "one of the largest effect sizes of any large-sample study covered in this review"), not "0.18-0.29+" as the claim states. 0.29 SD appears in the review as one specific intermediate finding (a homework-support program, stronger for at-or-below-median students), not the review's reported ceiling. The claim's "+" symbol may have been intended to signal "and beyond," but as a stated range it understates the review's own top-line figure by more than 2×.

## Status: VERIFIED
Full text read directly; the "mixed and often null outside a handful of well-run RCTs" characterization is confirmed verbatim (the Department of Education multi-program studies found "a general pattern of no effects" while a few specific well-run RCTs showed effects). One numeric correction: the review's own reported positive-effect ceiling is 0.63 SD (SimCalc), not ~0.29 SD as the claim's stated range implies — the claim should read "0.18 to 0.63 SD," which if anything makes the newer LLM-tutor effect sizes look less exceptional by comparison than the claim suggests.
