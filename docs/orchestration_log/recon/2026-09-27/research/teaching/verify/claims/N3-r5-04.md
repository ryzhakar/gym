# N3-r5-04 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
Handwritten expert error-message explanations outperformed both GPT-4-generated and conventional compiler error messages, on objective (time-to-fix) and subjective measures, for novice programmers debugging real C programs. https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.18661 (Santos & Becker, "Not the Silver Bullet," UKICER 2024; related DOI 10.1145/3689535.3689554).

## Primary text (quote, location)
Full PDF fetched (`arxiv.org/pdf/2409.18661`, pdftotext). Abstract: "GPT-4 generated error messages outperformed conventional compiler error messages in only 1 of the 6 tasks, measured by students' time-to-fix each problem. Handwritten explanations still outperform LLM and conventional error messages, both on objective and subjective measures."
Table 1 (§4.1, Tukey's HSD on log-transformed time-to-fix), Handwritten vs. GCC and vs. GPT-4, all 5 non-excluded tasks: Handwritten beats GCC in 4/5 tasks at p<0.05 (#const: 43.95s p=0.003; keyword-as-name: 63.42s p<0.001; missing curly brace: 122.3s p<0.001; reassigning constant: 53.0s p=0.031; missing parameter: 0.54s p=0.716, n.s.) and beats GPT-4 in all 5 (p≤0.029 in 4/5, p=0.157 n.s. for missing parameter). §4.2 (Figure 4, Likert): Handwritten rated "easy to understand" by 72–75% of participants across items, higher than GCC (14–31%) and GPT-4 (25–40%) on the reported items.

## Design facts
Within-subjects RCT, n=106 (of 113 recruited) intro-programming students, 6 buggy-C debugging tasks, 3 randomised/counterbalanced conditions per task (GCC control, handwritten expert explanation, GPT-4-0613-generated explanation). Outcome: time-to-fix (objective, log-transformed, one-way ANOVA + Tukey HSD per task) and post-task Likert ratings (subjective). No delay-to-test: outcome measured during the same session, immediately after each fix attempt.
