# N7-r1-07 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed

"In a randomized proctored experiment, AI access raised immediate test scores by 0.27 SD for undergraduates learning an unfamiliar topic, and the gain persisted at a one-week unaided follow-up test." DOI 10.48550/arxiv.2607.08849. Surveyed S3 R2 O2.

## Primary text (quote, location)

Contractor & Reyes, "Experimental Evidence on the Learning Impact of Generative AI" (arXiv:2607.08849v1, July 2026; identical text also circulating as IZA Discussion Paper No. 18792). Full text read from the arXiv PDF.

Abstract: "AI access raises immediate test scores by 0.27 standard deviations. These gains persist one week later."

Section 5.1 (Persistence of Effects on Test Scores), p.19-20: "The learning gains from AI access persist one week later (Table 4, columns 4–6) ... Control students' fraction of questions answered correctly falls from 56.3 percent in Session One to 49.5 percent in Session Two (p = 0.008). Treated students decline as well but remain ahead of the control group. The resulting ITT of β̂ = 5.1 pp (p = 0.027) is about 76 percent of the 6.7 pp Session One effect (though, given the standard errors, the two effects are not statistically distinguishable). Standardized, this corresponds to a β̂ = 0.27 SD effect."

Design (Introduction, p.2-3): "Our randomized experiment with 211 undergraduates unfolds over two sessions approximately one week apart ... We randomly assign students to an AI-allowed or an AI-forbidden condition ... In the second session, all students complete another test and write an essay on the same topic, this time without AI or any other resources." Timeline figure notes: "Session Two, conducted approximately one week later (mean of 6.99 days)."

## Design facts

| field | value |
|---|---|
| design | randomized, proctored, two-session field experiment (AI-allowed vs. AI-forbidden) |
| n | 211 undergraduates |
| population | undergraduate students, unfamiliar technical topic (institution: Middlebury College) |
| instrument | knowledge test (multiple-choice, 5 questions Session One / 10 questions Session Two) + graded analytical essay |
| delay-to-test | Session Two ≈ one week later (mean 6.99 days), completed fully unaided (no AI or other resources) |

Test-score result is statistically significant at retention (β̂ = 5.1 pp, p = 0.027); the claim's wording ("the gain persisted") is accurate and slightly conservative — the paper additionally reports the retained gain is not statistically distinguishable from the Session One gain in magnitude, which the claim doesn't state but doesn't contradict either.
