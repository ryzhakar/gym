## Claim as surveyed
Judgments of learning made shortly after (not immediately after) studying an item predict later recognition-test performance more accurately than judgments made immediately after study -- when a felt-learning probe is administered changes how much it should be trusted, independent of what is being measured.

## Primary text (quote, location)
DOI 10.1037/0022-0663.86.2.290 — Thiede & Dunlosky (1994), *Delaying students' metacognitive monitoring improves their accuracy in predicting their recognition performance*, Journal of Educational Psychology 86(2):290-302. **UNREACHABLE as full text**: this is a 1994 APA journal article; Unpaywall reports `is_oa: false`; Semantic Scholar reports `openAccessPdf.status: CLOSED`. The only text obtainable is the APA PsycNet abstract page.

Abstract (only text available): "The accuracy of students' judgments of learning (JOLs) in predicting recognition vs recall was investigated in 4 experiments... JOLs, which occurred either immediately after an item had been studied or shortly after an item had been studied... JOL accuracy was greater for delayed JOLs than immediate JOLs, and the accuracy of JOLs was lower in predicting recognition than recall."
