## Claim as surveyed
That same meta-analysis (31 studies, 112 effect sizes, 2002-2026) puts the overall effect of digital pedagogical agents on second-language outcomes at a moderate-to-large, publication-bias-robust magnitude — a per-hour-relevant baseline for agent-mediated instruction generally, not LLM-specific.

## Primary text (quote, location)
DOI 10.3390/bs16091583 — same source as N7-r7-05. Full text obtained via PMC mirror (PMC13603702), same access route.

Abstract: "the effect of DPA on SLA outcomes is about 0.607 (Hedges' g = 0.607; 95% CI [0.45, 0.77]; p < 0.001)... Funnel plot inspection, Egger's test, and the trim-and-fill method all showed the presence of publication bias, yet the adjusted effect size remained robust (Hedges' g = 0.521)."

Results §4.2/§4.4 (full detail behind the abstract's summary): "The overall Hedges' g was 0.607, with a 95% CI [0.45, 0.77], p < 0.001, indicating a moderately large effect size (g > 0.50)... Egger's test confirmed the visual assessment from the funnel plot: intercept β0 = 1.87, SE = 0.43, t = 4.35, p < 0.001... The trim-and-fill method estimated that approximately 9 studies needed to be imputed... The corrected effect size was g = 0.521, 95% CI [0.36, 0.69]. While this corrected estimate is lower than the uncorrected g = 0.607, it remains within the medium effect range, and the lower bound of the 95% confidence interval is substantially above zero."

Every element of the claim is confirmed exactly: the 31-study/112-effect-size/2002–2026 corpus, the "moderate-to-large" magnitude (g=0.607, Cohen's benchmarks: medium=0.50, large=0.80 — so g=0.607 sits between medium and large, matching "moderate-to-large"), and "publication-bias-robust" (three converging bias-detection methods, corrected g=0.521 with CI excluding zero).

"Not LLM-specific" is also accurate and explicit in the source: DPAs here are "virtual teaching entities... with characteristics such as emotion and socialization" studied since 2002 — an umbrella category of embodied/animated pedagogical agents that predates and is broader than LLM-based tutors.
