# N6-r5-03 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
In a lab experiment (n=24-28 adults), giving the person a brief on-screen notification warning that an interruption was about to begin — letting them prepare before pausing — significantly reduced the number of errors made on the very first action taken after resuming the primary task, versus interruptions given with no warning (F(1,23)=4.70, p=.040, np2=.17); this benefit held for both high- and low-working-memory-capacity groups. DOI 10.1002/acp.4126.

## Primary text (quote, location)
Full text read (author's accepted manuscript via Osaka University OUKA repository, retrieved through `cache.py get`). Section 5.5.2 "Number of errors after interruptions", p.32:

> "We also found a significant interaction between target stimulus location and interruption onset management condition (F(1,23) = 5.58, p =.027, 𝜂𝑝2 = 0.20). Further analysis of the simple main effects revealed that, in the condition without interruption onset management, more errors occurred on the first button after the interruption than before (F(1,23) = 23.14, p <.001, 𝜂𝑝2 = 0.50). In addition, at the first button position after interruption, more errors occurred under the without interruption onset management condition than those under the interruption onset management condition (F(1,23) = 4.70, p =.040, 𝜂𝑝2 = 0.17; Figure 6)."

Section 5.6 Discussion, p.35-36, on the WMC-group question:

> "The objective of Experiment 2 was to determine whether individual differences in WMC is related to errors after interruptions and to examine whether the adverse effects of interruption can be mitigated by managing interruption onset, irrespective of individual differences in WMC. ... However, contrary to our hypothesis, we found that interruption onset management can reduce the negative impact of interruptions irrespective of WMC."
