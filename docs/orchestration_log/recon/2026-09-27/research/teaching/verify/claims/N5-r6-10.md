# N5-r6-10 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
The quiet-eye-trained residents were the only group whose knot-tying performance transferred intact into a high-anxiety condition (mean difference 18%, p<.05); traditionally trained residents' performance collapsed back toward pretest levels under the same stress manipulation (p>.05) — the expert behavioral marker transfers specifically under pressure, not only at rest. DOI 10.1016/j.surg.2014.05.004.

## Primary text (quote, location)
Same source and full text as N5-r6-03 (Unpaywall open-access copy). Abstract/Results:

> "...however, only the QET group maintained their knot tying performance in the high anxiety conditions (mean difference: QET = 18%; p < 0.05) with the TT group decreasing their performance close to pretest levels (p > 0.05)."

Results section (body):

> "The ANOVA revealed a group by condition interaction (F(4,72) = 2.78, p = 0.03, ηp2 = 0.13; see Figure 2)... While the QET group maintained performance in the high anxiety conditions, the TT group performance decreased to pretest levels. These results suggest that the QET group had developed a strategy that enabled them to maintain performance, even under high anxiety conditions, whereas the performance of the TT group deteriorated."

A separate manipulation check confirms the anxiety induction itself worked: "participants had less heart rate variability in the high anxiety condition (748 ± 26 ms) compared to the low anxiety condition (818 ± 29 ms) (F(1,18) = 36.25, p < 0.001, ηp2 = 0.67)... no between-group differences (p > 0.05)," ruling out a simple "TT group felt less anxious" confound.
