# N3-r3-04 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed

In a randomized online experiment, both human-tutor-authored and ChatGPT-generated algebra hints produced positive pre-to-post learning gains, but only the human-authored hints' gain was statistically significant; ChatGPT-hint learning gains were substantially and significantly lower than human-hint gains in both algebra topic areas tested.

## Primary text (quote, location)

Full text obtained: Pardos, Z.A., Bhandari, S. (2023). "Learning gain differences between ChatGPT and human tutor generated algebra hints." arXiv:2302.06871v1 [cs.CY]. PDF at https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.06871.

§5 Results, Table 2, and text: "For both the Elementary Algebra and Intermediate Algebra lessons, learning gains were higher in the control condition and statistically significantly separable from the experiment (both with p = 0.038). ... all experiments showed positive learning gains; however, they were statistically significant differences only between pre and post-test scores of the control condition (p = 0.0219 for Elementary Algebra and p = 0.0213 for Intermediate Algebra), but not the experiment condition (p = 0.1427 for Elementary Algebra and p = 0.7912 for Intermediate Algebra)."

Table 2 learning-gain values: Elementary control 24.63% vs. experiment 11.14%; Intermediate control 23.65% vs. experiment 1.7%.

## Design facts

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Design | RCT, 2x2 (hint source: human vs. ChatGPT; lesson: Elementary vs. Intermediate Algebra), random assignment |
| n | 77 completing participants (of 80 recruited) |
| Population | Amazon Mechanical Turk workers, ≥high-school education, "master" qualification |
| Instrument | 3-item pre-test / 3-item post-test (identical items), Mann-Whitney U (non-normal data per Shapiro-Wilk) |
| Delay-to-test | None — post-test immediately follows the 5-item acquisition phase in the same session |
