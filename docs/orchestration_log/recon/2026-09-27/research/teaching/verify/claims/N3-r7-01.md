# N3-r7-01 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
A structured AI-tutor protocol built on the same pedagogical principles as effective human tutoring produced roughly double the immediate post-test learning gain of an in-class active-learning session at matched time-on-task. DOI 10.1038/s41598-025-97652-6.

## Primary text (quote, location)
Same source and full text as N7-r6-03. Results:

> "The median learning gains for students, relative to the pre-test baseline (M = 2.75, N = 316), in the AI-tutored group were over double those for students in the in-class active learning group. We conducted a two-sample rank-sum (Mann–Whitney) test to compare the distribution of post-scores of the two groups. The analysis revealed a statistically significant difference (z = -5.6, p < 10⁻⁸)."

On time-on-task: "During a 75-minute period, the in-class students spent 15 minutes taking the pre- and post-tests; we assume 60 minutes spent on learning... The median time on task for students in the AI group was 49 minutes... 70% of students in the AI group spent less than 60 minutes on task... there was no correlation between the time on task and students' post-test scores." The paper explicitly controlled for time-on-task in its regression model (Table S1).

**Precision note:** "matched time-on-task" is an approximation, not an exact match — the AI group's median time (49 min) was somewhat *less* than the in-class group's assumed 60 minutes of instruction, and the paper reports no correlation between AI time-on-task and score rather than a literal head-to-head time match. If anything this strengthens rather than weakens the claim (larger gains in somewhat less median time), but "matched" overstates the precision of the comparison.

## Design facts (table)

| design | randomized controlled experiment, 2-week crossover; AI tutor built on active-learning pedagogical principles vs. in-class active learning |
