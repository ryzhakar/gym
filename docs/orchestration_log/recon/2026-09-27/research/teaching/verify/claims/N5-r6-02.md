# N5-r6-02 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
"Novices shown an expert's gaze video and coached to mimic it more than halved simulator task completion time after 10 trials (55% reduction) versus movement-focused training (32%) or unguided discovery learning (39%) starting from an equal baseline." (DOI 10.1007/s00464-011-1802-2)

## Primary text (quote, location)
Full text fetched (Vine, Masters, McGrath, Bright & Wilson, *Surgical Endoscopy* 2012).

Results, Hypothesis 2 (control): "After only ten trials of learning, the gaze-trained group revealed a performance advantage over the other two groups. They more than halved their completion times from the baseline condition (55% reduction compared with 32% reduction for the movement group and 39% reduction for the discovery group; Fig. 1)."

Baseline equality confirmed: "ANOVA revealed no significant differences between the three groups for completion time, F(2,27) = 0.019, p = 0.982... or baseline tone counting performance, F(2,27) = 0.484, p = 0.622." Exact match to the claim.

## Design facts

| field | value |
|---|---|
| design | randomized 3-arm experiment (GAZE / MOVE / DISCOVERY), between-subjects |
| n | 30 medical trainees with no laparoscopic experience, 10 per group |
| population | novice medical trainees learning a laparoscopic eye-hand coordination task |
| instrument | completion time on LAP Mentor VR simulator "eye-hand coordination" task, 10 practice trials + a no-feedback control test |
| delay-to-test | none — control test given immediately after the 10-trial training block, same session |
