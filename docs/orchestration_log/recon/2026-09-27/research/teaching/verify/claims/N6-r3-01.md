## Claim as surveyed
"In 82 adult volunteers tracking a self-chosen daily habit (e.g. eating, drinking, activity) for up to 84 days with a self-report automaticity index, individual asymptotic-curve model fits show a median of 66 days (range 18-254) to reach 95% of one's automaticity plateau -- a very long and highly variable runway before a habit becomes self-sustaining." (DOI 10.1002/ejsp.674)

Best available primary-source text: the authors' abstract, recovered from a Wayback Machine capture of the Wiley abstract page:

"To investigate the process of habit formation in everyday life, 96 volunteers chose an eating, drinking or activity behaviour to carry out daily in the same context... for 12 weeks. They completed the self-report habit index (SRHI) each day... The majority (82) of participants provided sufficient data for analysis... Nonlinear regressions fitted an asymptotic curve to each individual's automaticity scores over the 84 days. The model fitted for 62 individuals, of whom 39 showed a good fit. Performing the behaviour more consistently was associated with better model fit. The time it took participants to reach 95% of their asymptote of automaticity ranged from 18 to 254 days... Missing one opportunity to perform the behaviour did not materially affect the habit formation process."

The abstract confirms: 82 of 96 volunteers analyzed, 84-day tracking window, SRHI-based automaticity index, asymptotic-curve modelling, and the 18–254 day range to 95% asymptote. It does **not** state the "median of 66 days" figure the claim asserts — that specific number is absent from every text this search could reach. It is a widely-repeated secondary-source figure (popularized as "66 days to form a habit") but its presence in the primary text (a median, as opposed to a mean or some other central-tendency statistic, over which of the 39/62/82-N subsets) could not be confirmed from any source read.
