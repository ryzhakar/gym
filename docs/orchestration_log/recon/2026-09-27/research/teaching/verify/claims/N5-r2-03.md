## Claim as surveyed

"The paper's own framing: 'inflexibility of thought induced by prior knowledge was shown by experts but the more expert they were, the less prone they were to the effect' -- expertise buys partial, not full, resistance to this specific failure mode." DOI 10.1016/j.cogpsych.2007.02.001. Surveyed S3 R2 O1.

## Primary text (quote, location)

Bilalić, McLeod & Gobet (2008), Cognitive Psychology 56(2). Full text read (Brunel repository PDF via Unpaywall).

Abstract (p.2 of PDF), verbatim: "The more familiar solution induced the Einstellung (set) effect even in experts, preventing them from finding the optimal solution. The presence of the non-optimal solution reduced experts' problem solving ability... to about that of players three standard deviations lower in skill level... Inflexibility of thought induced by prior knowledge (i.e., the blocking effect of the familiar solution) was shown by experts but the more expert they were, the less prone they were to the effect. Inflexibility of experts is both reality and myth. But the greater the level of expertise, the more of a myth it becomes."

Quote is word-for-word exact (the survey's clause is a substring of the abstract sentence, no paraphrase). "Expertise buys partial, not full, resistance" is a fair restatement of the abstract's own closing line: expertise reduces but (per the body, e.g. Masters at 18%, only "Grand Masters" at 100%) does not eliminate susceptibility until the most extreme skill band.
