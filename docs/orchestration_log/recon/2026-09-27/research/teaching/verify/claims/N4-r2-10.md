# N4-r2-10 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
Two lab experiments found that learners' self-assessment accuracy after studying worked examples (how well confidence in having understood the solution matched actual problem-solving performance) depended on which cue they were prompted to base that self-assessment on: performance-based cues produced better self-assessment accuracy than explanation-based cues, and telling learners in advance which cue to monitor improved accuracy further.

Attributed to DOI 10.1007/s10648-024-09944-4 (Waldeyer, Endres, Roelle, Baars, Renkl, "How to Optimize Self-Assessment Accuracy in Cognitive Skill Acquisition When Learning from Worked Examples," Educational Psychology Review, 2024). Survey note flagged this as abstract-only due to publisher bot-detection; full text obtained here via the author's institutional repository copy (Ruhr University Bochum), superseding the abstract-only read.

## Primary text (quote, location)
Full text: Unpaywall CC-BY OA copy, Ruhr University Bochum repository, `https://hss-opus.ub.ruhr-uni-bochum.de/opus4/files/11774/WaldeyerJulia07092024.pdf` (31 pp., published version, open access).

Abstract, p.103 (matches claim verbatim):

> "In Experiment 1, we tested whether self-assessment accuracy depended on whether learners were prompted to infer their self-assessments from explanation-based cues (ability to explain the problems' solutions) or from performance-based cues (problem-solving performance) and on whether learners were informed about the to-be-monitored cue before or only after the problem-solving phase. We found that performance-based cues resulted in better self-assessment accuracy and that informing learners about the to-be-monitored cue before problem-solving enhanced self-assessment accuracy. In Experiment 2, we again tested whether self-assessment accuracy depended on whether learners were prompted to infer their self-assessments from explanation- or performance-based cues. ... When learners received no further instructional support, like in Experiment 1, performance-based cues yielded better self-assessment accuracy."

## Design facts

| field | value |
|---|---|
| design | two independent lab experiments, randomized between-subjects factorial designs (Exp 1: 2×2; Exp 2: 2×2×2), a priori power analysis for each |
| n | Exp 1: N=135 German university students (final analytic sample after exclusions); Exp 2: N=252 (recruited 268, excluded for dyscalculia/ceiling pretest) |
| population | adult university students, various majors, learning mathematical (stochastics/urn-model) problem-solving via worked examples — a single-session lab task, not an applied professional/CS-adjacent skill domain |
| instrument | posttest of 10 stochastics problems (isomorphic, transfer, and abstract-understanding items); self-assessment accuracy = squared deviation (Schraw, 2009) between self-rated understanding and posttest performance on the isomorphic-item subset |
| delay-to-test | none — posttest administered in the same online session immediately after the problem-solving phase; authors flag this as a limitation and call for testing with a delay in future work |

## Status: VERIFIED

One precision correction to the claim: "Two lab experiments found ... telling learners in advance which cue to monitor improved accuracy further" overstates scope. The timing manipulation (informed before vs. after problem-solving) was tested and found significant only in Experiment 1. In Experiment 2, all participants were informed about the to-be-monitored cue in advance by design — that factor was held constant, not re-tested or replicated. The performance-vs-explanation-cue finding is genuinely supported by both experiments (in Experiment 2's baseline/no-support condition); the "informing in advance" finding rests on Experiment 1 alone.
