# N6-r7-04 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
Using experience-sampling and network analysis, the same study found an asymmetric co-occurrence pattern: moments of felt curiosity were highly likely to co-occur with felt interest, but moments of felt interest did not reliably co-occur with felt curiosity — interest, not curiosity alone, is the more consistently-present state, so a channel built only to spark curiosity risks missing the state that actually sustains engagement. DOI 10.31234/osf.io/wfprn.

## Primary text (quote, location)
Same full-text PDF as N6-r7-03 (fetched directly from OSF this round). Study 2 (co-occurrence network) results:

> "When feeling of curiosity occurred, feelings of interest (edge = 658; 88.8%) ... typically occurred at the same time ... It is important to note that when feelings of interest occurred, feelings of curiosity co-occurred only at a probability of 0.55 (edge = 658; 54.65%)."

Study 3 (a second, independent experience-sampling dataset within the same preprint) replicates the asymmetry with a different sample:

> "When feelings of curiosity occurred, feelings of ... interest (edge = 1691; 86.54%) ... typically occurred at the same time... When feelings of interest occurred, curiosity co-occurred at a probability of 0.72 (edge = 1691; 72.45%)."

Discussion:

> "That is, when students reported feeling curious, they were very likely to report feeling interested at the same time, but when they reported feeling interested, they only felt curious half of the time."

The asymmetric pattern (curiosity→interest co-occurrence 86.5-88.8%; interest→curiosity co-occurrence only 54.65-72.45%) is confirmed exactly, and — beyond what the claim itself states — replicated across two independent experience-sampling studies within this preprint, not just one.
