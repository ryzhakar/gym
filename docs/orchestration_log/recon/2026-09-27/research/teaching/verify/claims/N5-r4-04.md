# N5-r4-04 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed

Reviewers with higher commit counts (more experienced authors) were both more likely to give useful comments and significantly less likely to give invalid ones, but the authors flag this concentrates review load: "experienced authors of a project are the best reviewers...this approach has drawbacks due to knowledge centralization as well as overloading experienced contributors."

## Primary text (quote, location)

Turzo & Bosu (arXiv:2302.11686v2), Discussion, "Recommendation to authors" (p.19):

> "The results of our study also suggest that the likelihood of useful reviews increases with the reviewer's commit count. Therefore, experienced authors of a project are the best reviewers, who not only provide useful feedback but also are significantly less likely to write invalid ones. However, this approach has drawbacks due to knowledge centralization as well as overloading experienced contributors. Therefore, we recommend adding such contributors as reviewers for critical code changes."

Quote matches verbatim, location correct.

## Design facts

Same study/sample as N5-r4-01. Two statistical results back the two halves of the claim:
- "More useful comments": Table 7, log RCE β=1.34e-01, p=0.0001*** (RQ2.A usefulness-score model — same result as N5-r4-01).
- "Less likely invalid": Table 8 (RQ2.B, defect-category MLR model), log RCE under 'False Positive' column: OR=0.84, p=0.022* — a significant *reduction* in the odds of producing a 'False Positive' (invalid) comment as reviewer commit-derived coding experience (RCE) increases.
