# N5-r6-01 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
Explicit instruction in the expert's quiet-eye gaze pattern (long fixation before/through the stroke), added on top of ordinary practice, improved already-elite golfers' putting beyond baseline: QE duration alone predicted 43% of the variance in putting performance.

## Primary text (quote, location)
DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2011.00008 — Vine, Moore & Wilson (2011), *Quiet eye training facilitates competitive putting performance in elite golfers*, Front. Psychology 2:8. Full text fetched (Frontiers, open access).

Abstract: "QE duration predicted 43% of the variance in putting performance, underlying its critical role in the visuomotor control of putting."

Results, Regression analysis (p.5): "Results from the regression analysis revealed that QE duration predicted 43% of the variance in average performance error during the test phase (R2 = 0.43, β = 13.93, p < 0.005)."

Note: the number is precisely for average performance error (distance from hole on the retention/pressure tests), which the abstract compresses to "putting performance" — the survey's quote follows the abstract's own wording, not an overstatement introduced by the surveyor.

## Design facts
| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Design | RCT: 22 elite golfers randomly assigned to QE-trained vs. Control |
| n | 22 (male elite golfers, mean handicap 2.78) |
| Population | Adults, elite amateur golfers |
| Instrument | Mobile eye tracker (QE duration, ms); putts holed (%); performance error (cm) |
| Delay to test | Lab retention/pressure test after training + a further 10 competitive rounds over up to 3 months; regression run on pooled retention+pressure test-phase data |
