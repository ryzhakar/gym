# N2-r5-04 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
This meta-analysis explicitly declined to test whether a learner's existing skill level (novice vs. experienced) moderates the interleaved-practice transfer benefit, despite having skill-labeled subgroup data available -- an acknowledged evidence gap on how much prior knowledge the method presumes. DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1377122.

## Primary text (quote, location)
Results, "The CI effect in novice vs. experienced participants — comparison characteristics" (p.6-7): "The primary studies in the current review referred to different inclusion standards for a skilled group. Classifying skill (experience) levels correctly could lead our review in a different direction, still not warranting there will be no confusion or doubts. Therefore, we decided not to conduct the subgroup analysis of skilled versus novice." Same section documents that skill-labeled data existed: "In the immediate transfer articles, 90 participants were described as skilled... Out of the studies on delayed transfer, we classified 62 participants as experienced," with example descriptions ("intermediate-level junior tennis players," "9.5 years of experience in competitive baseball," "less than two years' basketball playing experience").

## Design facts
Same meta-analysis. The skill/experience subgroup was planned (following Brady 2004's precedent, explicitly named as a secondary objective in the Introduction: "3) To estimate the CI effect in novice vs. experienced participants") but not executed, by the authors' own decision, due to inconsistent skill-labeling standards across primary studies.

## Retraction check
Same as N2-r5-01 — not retracted.

## Replications & critics
Same citing set as N2-r5-01 — no source found that specifically fills this novice-vs-experienced gap for CI-on-transfer.

## Grade (S R O, changed from survey? why)
S=4, R=2, O=2 — unchanged from survey; this is a documented methodological decision within the S4 meta-analysis, not a separate weaker source.
