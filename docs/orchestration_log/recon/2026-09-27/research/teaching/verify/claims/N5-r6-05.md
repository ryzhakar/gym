# N5-r6-05 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
An intelligent tutoring system explicitly engineered to reproduce expert human tutors' dialogue moves reached the same immediate-post-test effect size as the domain-expert humans it modeled (ITS d=.71, expert human tutors d=.66), both far above the ~.4 median effect size across tutoring meta-analyses.

## Primary text (quote, location)
DOI 10.48550/arxiv.2504.16132 — Olney, D'Mello, Person et al., *Efficacy of a Computer Tutor that Models Expert Human Tutors* (arXiv preprint). Full text fetched.

Abstract: "Analysis using logistic mixed-effects modeling indicates significant positive effects on the immediate post-test for the ITS (d = .71) and human tutors (d = .66) which are in the 99th percentile of meta-analytic effects."

Introduction (p.1-2): "The median effect size (ES) across these meta-analyses is .4 over conventional instruction."

## Design facts
| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Design | Quasi-experiment, repeated-measures, 3-condition (ITS / Human tutor / Class-only), 9-week in-school study |
| n | 34 tenth-graders, one US high school, single Biology I class |
| Population | K-12 (10th grade), not adults — matches the survey's own R=1 tag |
| Instrument | 12-item pre/post multiple-choice knowledge tests per session; 48-item delayed post-test |
| Delay to test | Immediate post-test same session; delayed post-test 1-2 weeks later (see N5-r6-07) |

**Correction to the claim's framing.** The claim calls the Human condition "the domain-expert humans [the ITS] modeled." That conflates two distinct groups. The paper's own abstract distinguishes them: the Human condition in this RCT-like comparison is "human tutors who were experts in the domain but not in tutoring" — undergraduate biology majors/recent graduates given one day of training (Sec. 3.4, p.9). The ITS ("Guru"), by contrast, was built from "dialogue between 39 students and 10 expert tutors" (Sec. 2, p.3) — a separate corpus from a prior study, not from the Human-condition tutors in this trial. So Guru's effect size (d=.71) is not shown to match "the humans it modeled" — it is shown to match a different group of domain-expert-but-tutoring-novice comparison tutors. The raw statistics the claim cites (d=.71 ITS, d=.66 Human, both above the .4 meta-analytic median) are exact and correctly quoted; the causal/identity framing around "modeled" is not supported by the text and should be dropped or rewritten as "matched a domain-expert (but tutoring-inexperienced) human comparison condition" rather than "the humans it modeled."
