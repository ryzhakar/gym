# N6-r6-04 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
Retention gained from spacing practice sessions is not a function of the absolute gap between study sessions, but of the ratio between that gap and how long the skill must later be retained — a scheduling rule the daily-vs-weekly burst question can be read against directly.

## Primary text (quote, location)
DOI 10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354 — Cepeda, Pashler, Vul, Wixted & Rohrer (2006), *Distributed Practice in Verbal Recall Tasks: A Review and Quantitative Synthesis*, Psychological Bulletin 132(3):354-380. Full text fetched (this round; survey had flagged it as "not independently re-quoted from primary text").

Abstract: "Analyses suggest that ISI [interstudy interval] and retention interval operate jointly to affect final-test retention; specifically, the ISI producing maximal retention increased as retention interval increased."

Discussion, p.??: "Recent simulations (see Cepeda et al., 2005) demonstrate that ... optimal ISI increases in a predictable fashion as retention interval increases (with the optimal ratio of ISI to retention interval decreasing as retention interval itself grows)."

Note on strength of support: the empirical meta-analytic finding (abstract) is "optimal ISI increases as retention interval increases" — this supports the claim's core point (gap should scale with delay, not be fixed). The explicit "ratio" language, however, is attributed to a simulation model (Cepeda et al., 2005) discussed in the paper, not directly measured from the 839 meta-analyzed effect sizes. The claim's "ratio" framing is accurate to what the paper states, but that specific formulation is model-derived rather than a direct empirical measurement.
