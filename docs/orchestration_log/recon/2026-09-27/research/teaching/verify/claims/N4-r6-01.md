# N4-r6-01 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
"Across 35 studies training diagnostic competence in medical and teacher-education learners, instructional support produced a moderate positive effect on diagnostic-competence outcomes (Hedges' g=.39), and problem-solving-based practice helped learners at both lower and higher prior-knowledge levels, though the most effective scaffolding differed by level: high-guidance scaffolds helped less-advanced learners more, high-self-regulation scaffolds helped more-advanced learners more." (DOI 10.1007/s10648-019-09492-2)

## Primary text (quote, location)
Full text: paywalled (Springer *Educational Psychology Review*; no OA route found). Abstract only:

"This meta-analysis of 35 empirical studies builds on a conceptual framework and investigates the role of problem-solving, scaffolding, and context to foster diagnostic competences in learners with lower and higher professional knowledge bases... Instructional support has a moderate positive effect (g = .39; CI [.22; .56]; p = .001). Diagnostic competences are facilitated effectively through problem-solving independent of the learners' knowledge base. Scaffolding types providing high levels of guidance are more effective for less advanced learners, whereas scaffolding types relying on high levels of self-regulation are more effective for advanced learners."

Exact match to the claim, including the g=.39 figure and the guidance/self-regulation by prior-knowledge-level interaction.
