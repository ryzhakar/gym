# N2-r7-03 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
The inter-study interval that maximizes retention is not fixed; it scales up as the required retention interval scales up, so a single best spacing schedule for a practice unit does not exist independent of the durability target.

## Primary text (quote, location)
DOI 10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354 — Cepeda, Pashler, Vul, Wixted & Rohrer (2006), *Distributed Practice in Verbal Recall Tasks*, Psychological Bulletin 132(3):354-380. Full text already cached from an earlier claim in this round (N6-r6-04); re-used here per instruction.

Abstract (p.354): "Analyses suggest that ISI and retention interval operate jointly to affect final-test retention; specifically, the ISI producing maximal retention increased as retention interval increased."

This is the paper's own headline empirical finding (not a simulation-derived detail, unlike the more specific "ratio" language discussed for N6-r6-04) — directly and fully supports the claim: there is no single fixed optimal ISI; the optimal ISI itself is a function of how long retention must last.

## Design facts
Meta-analysis: 839 assessments of distributed practice across 317 experiments in 184 articles, joint analysis of interstudy interval (ISI) and retention interval (RI) as predictors of final-test accuracy.
