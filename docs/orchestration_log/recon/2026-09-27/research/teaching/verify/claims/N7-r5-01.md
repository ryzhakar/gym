# N7-r5-01 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
"An AI-driven real-time coaching system produced significantly higher simulated-surgery scores than human-instructor feedback by the final (5th) repetition." (DOI 10.1093/bjs/znad241.142)

## Primary text (quote, location)
The cited DOI is a *British Journal of Surgery* conference abstract (SP11.14, Association of Surgeons of Great Britain and Ireland, 2023): "Compared with baseline, groups 2 and 3 significantly improved in performance by the third and second repetition, respectively (p<0.01 and p=0.01). Between-groups comparison revealed significantly higher scores and extrinsic load for group 2 than group 3 in the fifth repetition." This is abstract-only — no full paper exists under this DOI (OpenAlex `type: conference-abstract`; no fuller Crossref/bibliographic match found for this exact abstract).

However, the same lab (Del Maestro group, McGill/NeuroVR brain-tumor-resection simulator) published what is, on every checked design detail, the full peer-reviewed version of this trial: Yilmaz, Tee et al., *Scientific Reports* 2024, `10.1038/s41598-024-65716-8`, "Real-Time multifaceted artificial intelligence vs In-Person instruction in teaching surgical technical skills." Full text fetched (PMC-hosted open-access). It reports, with identical group numbering (Group 1 = post-hoc feedback, Group 2 = real-time AI/ICEMS, Group 3 = real-time expert instruction):

"In the fifth task, the ICEMS group outperformed both post hoc and expert instruction groups, .266, 95% CI [.107 .425], p < .001 and .332, 95% CI [.173 .491], p = .005, respectively."

This directly confirms the claim (AI/Group 2 > human-instructor/Group 3 at the fifth repetition, p=.005) with an exact numeric match to the abstract's own group-2-vs-group-3 comparison.

## Design facts

| field | value |
|---|---|
| design | 3-arm parallel RCT (post-hoc feedback / real-time AI feedback / real-time human expert instruction) |
| n | Sci Rep full paper: 99 enrolled, 97 analyzed (32/33/32 per arm); BJS abstract states "ninety-eight" (preliminary/rounding discrepancy between conference-stage and final-paper enrollment, consistent with the same trial reported at two stages) |
| population | medical students, 4 Quebec medical schools, mean age 21.3 |
| instrument | ICEMS composite performance score (machine-scored, −1 to +1) on a virtual-reality brain-tumor-resection simulator (NeuroVR), 5 practice repetitions + 1 realistic task; blinded expert OSATS rating as secondary instrument |
| delay-to-test | none — single session, no follow-up ("Participant simulation performance data was recorded in one session without a follow-up") |
