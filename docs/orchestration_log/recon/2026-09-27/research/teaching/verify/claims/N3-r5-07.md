# N3-r5-07 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
Needle-handling was the one sub-task where e-feedback produced a significantly better improvement score than standard verbal feedback; the other assessed sub-tasks showed no significant difference by feedback mode. DOI 10.1016/j.jsurg.2016.11.011.

## Primary text (quote, location)
Abstract: "The mean improvement scores for the specific tasks were also not significantly different between the two groups except for the mean improvement score of needle handling, which was significantly better in the e-feedback group." Results section, near-identical sentence: "there was no significant difference between group 1 and 2 in mean improvement of all the domains except needle handling, which was significantly better in group 1 compared to group 2 (0.895 versus 0.5, respectively, P = 0.197)."

**Internal inconsistency in the source, reported as found, not resolved**: the Results-section sentence calls the needle-handling difference "significantly better" while reporting P = 0.197 for it — not significant at the conventional α = 0.05 the paper uses everywhere else (contrast e.g. P = 0.031 or P < 0.001 elsewhere in the same table). Table 1's "Needle handled with instruments" row carries a footnote-style symbol next to the e-feedback improvement figure (0.895) that the OCR renders as garbled characters ("ゅゆ"), consistent with the paper's own footnote key for "P < 0.05, unpaired t-test between group 1 and group 2" — i.e., the table's own symbol contradicts the P = 0.197 given in the running Results text for the same comparison. No cleaner copy of the typeset PDF could be retrieved (publisher-hosted repository copy at kar.kent.ac.uk timed out with a 504 on refetch); this is reported as-is from the only full text available.

## Design facts
Same trial as N3-r5-06: blinded RCT, n=37 analysed, task-level (sub-task) improvement scores on a validated pro forma, 2-day retest.

## Retraction check
Same as N3-r5-06 — not retracted.

## Replications & critics
Same citing set as N3-r5-06 (41 total) — no source found that specifically re-examines the needle-handling sub-task result.

## Grade (S R O, changed from survey? why)
S=2, R=2, O=1 — S downgraded from the survey's 3 to 2: the specific numeric claim (needle handling "significantly better") is undermined by the source's own reported p-value for that exact comparison, so this sub-finding is weaker evidence than the trial's main (S3) result even though it comes from the same RCT.

## Status: REFUTED (changed from UNVERIFIABLE)
Changed from UNVERIFIABLE: the claim asserts a significant result; the full primary text was read and its Results section reports p=0.197 for that exact comparison — not significant. A full text that contradicts the claim's own number is a refutation from the primary source, not an unreachable-text case; the abstract's looser wording and the table's ambiguous OCR'd symbol do not outweigh the Results section's explicit p-value.
