# N3-r3-02 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed

In a university ITS lab course, making hints available at no scoring penalty versus penalizing hint use produced statistically significant differences in how often students requested hints, but only for exercises where baseline hint-request rates were neither very high nor very low; the effect was moderated by problem type and difficulty, not uniform across all exercises.

Full text obtained: Muñoz-Merino, P.J., Delgado Kloos, C., Muñoz-Organero, M. (2010). "Behavior Effect of Hint Selection Penalties and Availability in an Intelligent Tutoring System." ITS 2010, LNCS 6095, pp. 384-386. Springer-Verlag. PDF at e-Archivo (UC3M repository): https://e-archivo.uc3m.es/bitstreams/29640ca2-5daa-4679-9a14-958276b40aff/download (resolved from Unpaywall best_oa_location http://hdl.handle.net/10016/19175).

§3 Results and Analysis: "From table 1, there is a significant difference between the number of hints selected, comparing the cases of having penalties or not, when the amount of selected hints is not too high nor too low. As MC problems make the number of requested hints to be low then none of the cases resulted in a significant difference. ... if it is easy or medium, then the number of requested hints will be medium and there will be significant difference."

§4 Conclusions: "There were several statistically significant differences in the number of hint requests between the hinting technique of having penalties for selecting hints or not, depending on the type and difficulty of the problem."

## Design facts

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Design | Within-course field experiment, exercise-level random assignment to hinting technique (not a lab RCT on subjects; balanced randomization of technique per exercise instance) |
| n | 12 exercises, 8 comparing penalty vs. no-penalty (per-exercise interaction counts 36-48 each); 4 comparing direct availability vs. not (28-46 each) |
| Population | Students in "two editions of a computer architecture laboratory course" (university course) |
| Instrument | Binomial 95% CI on probability of requesting ≥1 hint per exercise; non-overlap of CIs = significance |
| Outcome measured | Hint-request behavior/frequency only — no learning-outcome or test-score measure reported anywhere in the paper |
| Delay-to-test | N/A — no test administered; behavioral logging only |
