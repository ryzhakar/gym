# N7-r3-04c — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
Corrected replacement for N7-r3-04: "Among students in a 6-week graduate robotics course, group membership in the Socratic-Guidance (SG) versus Prompt-Refinement (PR) LLM-tutor condition predicted adoption of 'understanding-driven' prompting during a later unconstrained-LLM course project: 13 of 29 SG-condition students vs 4 of 23 PR-condition students (chi-sq=8.14, p=.017); groups with more understanding-driven prompters scored higher on project understanding (beta=1.30, p=.025)." DOI 10.48550/arxiv.2607.03303.

## Primary text (quote, location)
Full text: `https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.03303v1`, §4.1 and §4.4.

- §4.1 (post-intervention project-phase cluster analysis, p.10-11): "A cluster analysis of project prompts... identified three prompting patterns... (i) Implementers with high-quality prompts (n = 25), (ii) Understanding-driven prompters with high-quality prompts (n = 17), and (iii) Debuggers with low-quality prompts (n = 10)." (25+17+10 = 52 total, matching the abstract's "52 students.")
- §4.4: "the distribution of students across clusters differed significantly by condition (χ2 = 8.14, p = 0.017). While the number of Implementers was similar across conditions (nSG = 14, nPR = 11), students from the SG condition were more likely to enact Understanding-driven prompting patterns (nSG = 13, nPR = 4), whereas students from the PR condition were more likely to enact Debugger patterns (nPR = 8, nSG = 2)."
- Arithmetic check: SG total = 14 (Implementers) + 13 (Understanding-driven) + 2 (Debuggers) = **29**. PR total = 11 + 4 + 8 = **23**. 29+23 = 52, consistent with the paper's overall total.
- §4.4 (group-level understanding): "Groups with a higher number of Understanding-driven prompters achieved significantly higher understanding grades than groups with more Debuggers (β = 1.30, p = 0.025)."
