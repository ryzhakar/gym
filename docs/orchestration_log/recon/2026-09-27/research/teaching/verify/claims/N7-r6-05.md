# N7-r6-05 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
In an exploratory RCT with expert tutors directly supervising and revising every LLM-drafted message, students supported by the supervised LLM were 5.5 percentage points more likely to solve a novel (transfer) problem correctly than students who got human-tutor-only guidance.

## Primary text (quote, location)
DOI 10.48550/arxiv.2512.23633 — LearnLM Team, Google & Eedi, *AI tutoring can safely and effectively support students: An exploratory RCT in UK classrooms* (arXiv preprint, 2025-12-29). Full text fetched.

Abstract: "students who received support from LearnLM were 5.5 percentage points more likely to solve novel problems on subsequent topics (with a success rate of 66.2%) than those who received tutoring from human tutors alone (rate of 60.7%)."

Results (p.4): "a supervised session with LearnLM increased the likelihood of learning transfer to a distinct topic by an additional 5.5 percentage points [–1.4%, +12.4%] relative to human tutoring... we attribute a high credibility (93.6%) to LearnLM offering better support for knowledge transfer than human tutors alone."

**Important caveat the claim omits.** The 5.5-point figure carries a 95% *credible* interval of [–1.4%, +12.4%] — it spans zero. The paper's own framing is Bayesian (posterior credibility, not a frequentist p-value): they report 93.6% posterior probability that LearnLM's transfer advantage is real, which is a real but sub-95% signal, not a conventionally "significant" result. The claim states the 5.5-point gain as if established without qualification; the source itself is more guarded ("high credibility," not certainty), and by 95%-CI convention this would not clear a significance threshold.

## Design facts
| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Design | Exploratory RCT, N=165 students, 5 UK secondary schools; students randomized to static hints vs. interactive tutoring, then (within tutoring) randomized per-session to human tutor vs. LearnLM (with N=17 expert tutors directly supervising/editing every LearnLM message) |
| n | 165 students, 17 supervising tutors |
| Population | K-12 (UK secondary school), not adults |
| Instrument | Eedi mathematics platform: first-question-correct on the following topic unit as the transfer measure |
| Delay to test | Immediate transfer to the next study unit within the same course sequence — not a ≥7-day delayed retention test |
