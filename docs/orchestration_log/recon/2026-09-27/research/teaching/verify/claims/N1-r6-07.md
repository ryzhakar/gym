# N1-r6-07 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
"Both training arms in the same trial showed statistically indistinguishable knowledge-test gains (mean difference=1.17, p=.017) despite the OSATS-based retention index diverging by 23 points - the low-cost declarative-knowledge proxy did not detect the procedural-skill difference that the costlier direct performance measure did." (DOI 10.7759/cureus.99178)

## Primary text (quote, location)
Full text (same source). Results/Table 1: "Across all participants, the mean knowledge scores improved from 6.80±2.14 at baseline to 7.97±1.03 post-intervention... mean difference=−1.17; 95% CI: −2.12 to −0.22; p=0.017." Discussion: "Both groups demonstrated significant improvements in knowledge... Both groups achieved significant knowledge gains (mean difference = 1.17; p = 0.017)." This is a pooled/whole-sample knowledge gain, reported once (not broken out per arm in the text I could find) — the claim's characterization ("both training arms... indistinguishable") reflects that the paper reports one combined knowledge-gain figure with no significant between-group knowledge difference reported anywhere in Results.

Retention-index divergence: SBML 114.0%, video 90.6% (Table 5) — a 23.4-point gap, matching the claim's "23 points."

## Design facts
Same trial as N1-r6-05/06. Knowledge instrument: validated 10-item MCQ, administered pre/post intervention (not at 2-week follow-up).

## Retraction check
Same as N1-r6-05: not retracted.
