# N1-r7-04 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
"Practice on a paired-associate task improved the correlation between learners' real-time judgments of learning and their actual recall (better resolution) while simultaneously making those same judgments more underconfident (worse calibration) -- the felt-learning signal's two components (does it track truth; is its absolute level right) move in opposite directions as practice accumulates, so a single self-monitoring probe cannot be trusted as a stable instrument across a training arc."
Source: 10.1037/0096-3445.126.4.349 (Koriat, 1997, JEP: General)

## Primary text (quote, location)
Abstract/Results: "Although practice impaired calibration, increasing underconfidence, it did improve resolution (i.e., the recall-JOL correlation)." (Abstract). Confirmed in body: "the differential effects of practice on... Calibration. The results presented so far indicate that the effects of repeated practice are stronger for recall than for JOLs... [with an] increase in underconfidence with increasing practice" (Results, Exp. 1, p.~5 of cached text) and "Resolution. The results presented so far indicate that increased practice with a list of items impairs the monitoring [gap]... practice seems to impair calibration while improving resolution" (Results, Exp. 1).
