# N6-r5-10 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
In a week-long experience-sampling study of 78 adult workers, the great majority of self-reported flow experiences (high challenge and high skill together) occurred during work, not leisure, and flow tracked with better quality of experience on nearly every measured dimension — except motivation: people reported feeling more motivated during leisure than during work regardless of flow state. Flow (a good in-the-moment experience) and motivation to continue the activity are empirically dissociable, not the same lever. DOI 10.1037/0022-3514.56.5.815.

Full text is closed access: Unpaywall (`is_oa: false`, `oa_status: closed`, no repository copy) and Semantic Scholar (`openAccessPdf.status: CLOSED`) both confirm no open-access route; this is a 1989 APA journal article, pre-dating PMC/arXiv deposit norms, and no author-hosted copy was located. What was read is APA PsycNet's structured abstract record:

> "Followed 78 adult workers for 1 week with the experience sampling method. ... The main question was whether the quality of experience was more influenced by whether a person was at work or at leisure or more influenced by whether a person was in flow (i.e., in a condition of high challenges and skills). Results showed that all the variables measuring the quality of experience, except for relaxation and motivation, are more affected by flow than by whether the respondent is working or in leisure. Moreover, the great majority of flow experiences are reported when working, not when in leisure. Regardless of the quality of experience, however, respondents are more motivated in leisure than in work."

This matches the claim closely, including the flow/motivation dissociation.
