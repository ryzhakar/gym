# N7-r7-12 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
The same review cites an AI computer-vision system that automatically and continuously derives an objective skill metric (dissection/exposure time ratio) from surgical video, cleanly separating novice from expert performance without a human rater — evidence that always-on automated measurement, not just always-on help, is feasible in a comparable professional-skill domain. DOI 10.1186/s12909-026-09687-6.

## Primary text (quote, location)
Same full text as N7-r7-11 (Europe PMC, CC BY). Results table (Sato et al. row):

> "Sato et al. (2025) [21] ... An AI-based computer vision model automatically extracted instrument status from surgical videos to measure dissection and exposure times... Novices and experts were compared by measuring dissection and exposure times automatically derived by the AI model... Exposure time (novice vs. expert): 195.7s vs. 89.7s (P < 0.001); D/E ratio: 0.174 vs. 0.315 (P = 0.003)."

Discussion, restating the same finding: "Sato et al. utilized an AI-based interface recognition model to automatically measure the 'dissection' and 'exposure' times in Robot-Assisted Radical Prostatectomy (RARP), finding that the exposure time of the expert group was shorter than that of the novice group (89.7 s vs. 195.7 s, p < 0.001)."

Both the exposure-time figures (195.7s novice vs. 89.7s expert) and the D/E-ratio figures (0.174 vs. 0.315, p=.003) match the claim exactly, and the underlying primary study is a computer-vision system with no human rater in the loop, as claimed.
