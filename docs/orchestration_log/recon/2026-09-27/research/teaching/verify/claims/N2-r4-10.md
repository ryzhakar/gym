## Replications & critics
OpenAlex `cites:W4396577662` surfaced a directly on-topic, independent, higher-evidence-tier source: Abdi Tabari, Lee & Farahanynia, "Impact of methodological differences in task sequencing on second language learning: a multi-level meta-analysis," DOI 10.1515/iral-2024-0219 (2024, published after Jin & Yan). Its abstract (Semantic Scholar, full text not fetched — also paywalled, not attempted further as it is context here, not the claim's own source):
> "Drawing on the SSARC model, TBLT research has investigated the role of task sequencing in L2 production, **yielding mixed results**. ... Task complexity emerged as a significant factor influencing L2 performance, with more complex tasks yielding larger effect sizes. Additionally, **simple and complex sequencing tasks exhibited a medium effect size in enhancing accuracy**, particularly in between-group studies."

This is a meaningful qualifier on the surveyed claim: an independent multi-level meta-analysis (n=16 studies through May 2024) describes task-sequencing research as yielding **mixed results** overall, with the positive effect specifically confirmed only for **accuracy** at a **medium** effect size in between-group designs — not the surveyed claim's broader "improved overall complexity/accuracy/fluency outcomes." The two reviews are not in direct contradiction (mixed-but-partially-positive is compatible with "has been shown to... improve CAF" read narrowly), but the meta-analysis does not support the survey's "reliably... overall improve CAF" framing for complexity and fluency specifically, only accuracy.
