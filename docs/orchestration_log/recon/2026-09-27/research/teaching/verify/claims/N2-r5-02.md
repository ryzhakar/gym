# N2-r5-02 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
The transfer benefit of interleaved (high-CI) over blocked practice is strongly age-moderated: negligible in young learners, medium in adults, large in older adults. DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1377122.

## Primary text (quote, location)
Abstract: "Age group analysis turned out to be significant only in adults and older adults. In both, the random practice was favored. In adults, the effect was medium (SMD = 0.54), whereas in older adults was large (SMD = 1.28). In young participants, the effect size was negligible (SMD = 0.12)." Results, "The CI effect in young vs. adults vs. elderly adults" (p.6-7): young subgroup "pooled effect size... was negligible SMD = 0.12 (95% CI: −0.28, 0.53; p = 0.54)"; adults subgroup "medium SMD = 0.54 (95% CI: 0.16, 0.92; p = 0.54)" [note: the paper itself reports p=0.54 here, apparently a copy-paste duplication from the young-subgroup line — see caveat]; older adults "large SMD = 1.28 (95% CI: −0.34, 2.90; p = 0.10)."

**Caveat, reported as found**: the paper's own text gives p = 0.54 for the "significant" adult-subgroup medium effect, identical to the p-value given one paragraph earlier for the negligible young-subgroup effect, and the older-adults SMD=1.28 result (p=0.10) is not conventionally significant despite being called "large" and the abstract stating age analysis "turned out to be significant only in adults and older adults." These are internal inconsistencies in the source's own reported p-values, not something this verification resolves.

## Design facts
Same meta-analysis as N2-r5-01. Age subgroups: young (<18y, 468 participants, 21 effect sizes), adults (18–59y, 812 participants, 55 effect sizes), older adults (60y+, 121 participants, 8 effect sizes) — 33 articles in this subgroup comparison.

## Grade (S R O, changed from survey? why)
S=3, R=2, O=2 — S downgraded from the survey's 4 to 3: the age-subgroup analysis (unlike the S4-worthy overall pooled analysis) carries internally inconsistent significance reporting (see caveat above) and small per-arm samples (8 effect sizes for older adults), weakening it below "meta-analysis reporting heterogeneity and moderators" cleanly.

The direction of the claim (negligible → medium → large across young → adult → older-adult) matches the source's abstract and Results exactly. Flagged: the source's own significance/p-value reporting for this specific subgroup breakdown is internally inconsistent (see caveat), which is disclosed rather than smoothed over.
