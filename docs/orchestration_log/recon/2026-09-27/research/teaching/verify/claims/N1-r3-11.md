# N1-r3-11 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
Two experiments (N=610, N=871) found a scalable, facilitator-free calibration-feedback training method (the Practical scoring rule) produced no improvement in confidence calibration over control or plain outcome-feedback, despite being designed for domain-general, low-cost deployment.

## Primary text (quote, location)
Martin, M., & Mandel, D. R. (2024). "Calibration Feedback With the Practical Scoring Rule Does Not Improve Calibration of Confidence." Futures & Foresight Science. DOI 10.1002/ffo2.199.

Full text read from the authors' PsyArXiv preprint, accepted version (`osf.io/dw89q`, files "Calibration_Training_Accepted_Version.pdf"), linked to the same DOI (Semantic Scholar records the DOI as `HYBRID`/CC-BY open access).

Abstract, p.1: "We found no improvement in calibration due to either training regime." Experiment 1 (p.9, line ~579): "resulted in a total of 610 North American participants." Experiment 1 result (p.11): "training condition, H(2, N = 610) = 1.98, p = .37. In sum, the results did not support the primary [hypothesis]." Experiment 2 (p.13): "After all data exclusions, 871 participants were retained." Experiment 2 result (p.15): "condition, H(2, N = 871) = 4.62, p = .10, contrary to the primary hypothesis."
