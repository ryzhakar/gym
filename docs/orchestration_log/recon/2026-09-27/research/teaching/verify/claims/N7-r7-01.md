# N7-r7-01 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
"Continuous exposure to AI-assisted colonoscopy is associated with a drop in endoscopists' unaided (non-AI) adenoma detection rate months later — a directly observed skill-durability harm from habitual AI assistance."
Source: 10.1016/s2468-1253(25)00133-5 (Budzyń et al., 2025, Lancet Gastroenterology & Hepatology — "Endoscopist deskilling risk after exposure to artificial intelligence in colonoscopy: a multicentre, observational study")

## Primary text (quote, location)
Abstract (only text obtained): "FINDINGS: Between Sept 8, 2021, and March 9, 2022, 1443 patients underwent non-AI assisted colonoscopy before (n=795) and after (n=648) the introduction of AI... The ADR of standard colonoscopy decreased significantly from 28·4% (226 of 795) before to 22·4% (145 of 648) after exposure to AI, corresponding with an absolute difference of -6·0% (95% CI -10·5 to -1·6; p=0·0089)."

## Replications & critics

## Grade (S R O, changed from survey? why)
S=3 (survey's grade) — the design is observational/before-after across centres rather than a randomized comparison; per the S table this is arguably closer to **S=2** ("large-N observational dataset with stated model" fits better than "RCT or quasi-experiment with control", since AI exposure here is a naturalistic rollout, not a randomized within-study manipulation of exposure per se — though the parent ACCEPT trial that produced the exposure schedule was itself randomized-by-date). Flagged as borderline S2/S3; leaving S=3 given the parent trial's randomization but noting the deskilling analysis itself is observational. R=2 (adults, professional/medical skill domain) — unchanged. O=2 (a real months-later delay, directly measuring unaided performance) — unchanged, this is the strongest O-grade evidence among this batch.
