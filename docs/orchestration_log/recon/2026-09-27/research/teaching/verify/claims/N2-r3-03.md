## Replications & critics
Semantic Scholar citations (300 of 491 citing works scanned, keyword-filtered):
- Wang, Zhang & Cheng (2026), "Can project-based learning improve students' mathematics learning outcomes: a three-level meta-analysis," Frontiers in Psychology, DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1868415 (open access, PMC13586001) — narrower domain (math PBL only), 33 studies/84 effect sizes, three-level meta-analytic model with outlier removal (Cook's distance). Overall d=0.529 [0.38, 0.68], but **publication-bias-corrected (trim-and-fill) estimate = 0.275** — roughly a third of Chen & Yang's pooled 0.71, with the authors explicitly cautioning "findings should be interpreted cautiously" given publication bias. This is a direct, methodologically more rigorous replication in a narrower slice of the same literature, and it materially tempers the magnitude (not the direction) of the achievement-gain claim.
- Farshad & Fortin (2026), "An umbrella review of meta-analyses on project-based learning," Educational Research Review, DOI 10.1016/j.edurev.2026.100809 — title indicates a direct re-synthesis of PBL meta-analyses (would very likely include this paper); abstract not retrievable (elided by publisher, Unpaywall closed).
No source found reversing the direction of the effect; the Wang et al. 2026 publication-bias correction is the strongest available caveat on effect-size magnitude.
