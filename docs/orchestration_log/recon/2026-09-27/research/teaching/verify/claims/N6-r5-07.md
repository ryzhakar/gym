# N6-r5-07 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
In a year-long quasi-experiment with 50 software professionals (25 given a gamified work portal, 25 working traditionally, across 5 IT organizations), the gamified group significantly exceeded the control group on every measured outcome, including new courses/technologies learned over the year (mean difference 2.56, t(48)=6.45, p<.001) and code quality (mean difference 17.72, t(48)=4.75, p<.001) — a rare direct test, in a CS-adjacent professional population, of whether a structured engagement mechanism sustains continued skill-relevant learning over real calendar time. DOI 10.7717/peerj-cs.2285.

## Primary text (quote, location)
Full text read (PMC/Europe PMC open-access copy, CC BY). Results section, Hypothesis 5 (new learning) and Hypothesis 3 (code quality):

> "This indicates that gamification indeed plays a role in fostering new learning among participants in the intervention group compared to those in the control group with a mean difference of 2.560 (t(48) = 6.454, p < 0.001, 95% CI [1.762, 3.358] ... )."

> "...the intervention group displayed significant improvement, evident in the mean difference of 17.720 (t(48) = 4.753, p < 0.001, 95% CI [10.224, 25.216]) as shown in Table 7."

Both statistics match the claim exactly (rounding: 6.454→6.45, 4.753→4.75). Discussion/Conclusion also states outcomes were superior "across all evaluated dimensions" (task completion, score, code quality, timeliness, new learning, team-task performance), matching "significantly exceeded the control group on every measured outcome."

## Design facts (table)
| field | value |
|---|---|
| design | quasi-experiment, intervention (gamified portal) vs. control group, 1-year field deployment in 5 IT organizations; independent-samples t-tests pre-specified per outcome |
| n | 50 software professionals (25 intervention / 25 control; 20 developers, 15 designers, 15 testers) |
| population | software professionals with ≥2 years' experience, across 5 IT organizations |
| instrument | task completion, task score, code quality (leader/CEO-rated), timeliness, new courses/technologies learned, team-task score — SPSS-analyzed |
| delay-to-test | 1 year (all outcomes measured across the full 1-year deployment, not immediate) |

Flags carried from the survey and confirmed on this read: assignment to intervention/control is not described as randomized (true quasi-experiment); the paper states CEOs "closely monitored" the environment during treatment, a plausible Hawthorne/social-desirability confound the paper does not address; code-quality is scored by team leaders/CEOs, not an objective instrument.
