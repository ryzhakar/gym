# N3-r5-08 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
Both e-feedback and standard verbal feedback were rated similarly high on acceptability by trainees (Likert 4.42 vs 4.71 of 5, not significant) -- a self-report finding that does not itself establish a learning-outcome difference between the two feedback modes. DOI 10.1016/j.jsurg.2016.11.011.

## Primary text (quote, location)
Abstract/Results (identical sentence in both): "From a maximum score of 5, both e-feedback and standard verbal feedback achieved high mean Likert grades as recorded by the participants (4.42 (range 2-5) and 4.71 (range 4-5) respectively; P= 0.274)." Free-text comments (Table 2) show mixed reactions to e-feedback (convenience vs. "hard to visualize written feedback," delayed recall of performance details) alongside positive comments for verbal feedback (in-person clarification, discussion) — consistent with a self-report/acceptability measure rather than a learning-outcome measure.

## Design facts
Same RCT as N3-r5-06/07. Acceptability instrument: 5-point Likert ("end of study questionnaire"), Mann-Whitney U used for group comparison of Likert scores per the Methods section.

## Retraction check
Same as N3-r5-06 — not retracted.

## Replications & critics
Same citing set as N3-r5-06 — no source specifically reanalyses the acceptability/Likert result.

## Grade (S R O, changed from survey? why)
S=3, R=2, O=0 — unchanged from survey. O=0 because this is a self-report/satisfaction outcome, not a performance measure (per the grading rubric, O=0 for "assisted performance, self-report, satisfaction, time-on-task only").
