# N5-r6-07 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
The expert-modeled ITS's and the expert human tutors' advantage over no-tutoring both persisted, attenuated, at a 1-2 week delayed post-test (ITS d=.36, human tutors d=.39) rather than washing out entirely.

## Primary text (quote, location)
DOI 10.48550/arxiv.2504.16132 — same source as N5-r6-05. Full text fetched.

Abstract: "as well as significant positive effects on the delayed post-test for the ITS (d = .36) and human tutors (d = .39)."

Results (p.11): "students were ... more likely to correctly answer questions on the post-test than the pre-test, OR = 3.13, d = .71, ... but less likely to correctly answer questions on the delayed [test], OR = 1.77, d = .36" (ITS); "... OR = 2.90, d = .66 ... pre-test, OR = 1.86, d = .39, but less likely to correctly answer questions" (Human tutor). Discussion (p.11): "The ITS had a slightly larger effect on pre- to post-test (d = .71) compared to Human (d = .66), and Human had a slightly larger effect on pre- to delayed test (d = .39) compared to ITS (d = .36)."

Design (Sec. 3.2): delayed post-test occurs "on the third week of a cycle" following two weeks of tutoring — i.e., 1-2 weeks after the tutoring sessions being tested, matching the claim's stated delay window.
