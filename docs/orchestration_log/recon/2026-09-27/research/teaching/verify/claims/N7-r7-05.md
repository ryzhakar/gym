# N7-r7-05 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
A three-level meta-analysis of digital pedagogical agents (a category LLM tutors extend) on second-language-acquisition outcomes finds the pooled effect does not generalize uniformly: it varies greatly by study design, cultural context and learner characteristics.

## Primary text (quote, location)
DOI 10.3390/bs16091583 — *The Impact of Pedagogical Agents on Second Language Acquisition Outcomes: A Meta-Analysis Guided by Social Agency Theory*, Behavioral Sciences 16(9):1583 (2026). `cache.py` returned abstract-only (MDPI's site returned an Akamai "Access Denied" edge block on direct fetch too); full text obtained instead via the article's PMC mirror (PMC13603702, found via Semantic Scholar's `openAccessPdf`/`externalIds`), which is the publisher's own gold-OA (CC-BY) version of record.

Abstract: "the magnitude of the effect varies greatly with different kinds of study designs, cultural contexts and learner characteristics, and although there is a moderating effect by publication type, it is not statistically significant."

Moderator analysis, Results §4.3 (confirms and quantifies the abstract's summary):
- Study design: "Longitudinal studies (g = 0.648, 95% CI [0.48, 0.82]) yielded significantly larger effect sizes than cross-sectional studies (g = 0.421, 95% CI [0.21, 0.63]), F = 5.83, p = 0.016."
- Cultural background: "Studies conducted in Eastern cultural contexts (g = 0.665...) exhibited stronger agent effects than those in Western contexts (g = 0.473...), F = 4.27, p = 0.039."
- Learner demographics: "Primary school students exhibited the largest effect size (g = 0.718...), followed by middle school students (g = 0.591), university students (g = 0.542), and non-student or other groups (g = 0.488)," F = 8.91, p = 0.031.
- Publication type: not significant (F = 2.14, p = 0.144) — matching the abstract's own caveat.

Note on framing: "digital pedagogical agents (a category LLM tutors extend)" is the survey's own analytical bridge to the LLM-tutoring research question — the source paper does not itself frame DPAs as an LLM-tutor precursor category; its studies (2002–2026) are overwhelmingly non-LLM embodied/animated agents. This framing is reasonable but should be read as the surveyor's interpretive gloss, not the source's claim.
