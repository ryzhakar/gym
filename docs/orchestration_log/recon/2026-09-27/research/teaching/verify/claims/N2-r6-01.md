# N2-r6-01 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
"Across 35 studies (33 after outlier removal, 84 effect sizes) in a preregistered three-level meta-analysis, project-based learning produced a significant overall effect on mathematics achievement (Cohen's d=0.529) relative to comparison instruction, but publication-bias correction (trim-and-fill) roughly halved the estimate to d=0.275, and the authors state this lower figure 'should be interpreted cautiously' only in the sense of flagging likely overestimation, not reversing the direction of the effect." (DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1868415)

## Primary text (quote, location)
Full text fetched (Frontiers in Psychology, open access, Wang, Zhang & Cheng, 2026; PROSPERO CRD420251105265).

Abstract: "a total of 35 eligible studies were included. In the primary meta-analytic model, two statistically outlying studies were identified and excluded based on Cook's distance. Ultimately, 33 studies with 84 effect sizes were incorporated into the main effect analysis... The results indicated a significant overall effect size for PBL (Cohen's d = 0.529, 95% CI [0.38, 0.68]), but publication bias analyses suggested potential overestimation (trim-and-fill corrected estimate = 0.275); therefore, findings should be interpreted cautiously."

Results, "Main effects and heterogeneity": "d = 0.529, 95% CI [0.38, 0.68], t = 7.14, p < 0.001... Q(83) = 659.185, p < 0.001." Results, publication-bias section: "The trim-and-fill method estimated 23 missing study effects... d = 0.275 (95% CI [0.157, 0.394])... The fail-safe N was 10,932, far exceeding [Rosenthal's criterion]." Exact match, including the "interpreted cautiously" framing being about the magnitude/overestimation, not the direction (the fail-safe N of 10,932 and the significant, non-zero trim-and-fill CI both still support a real, non-null, positive effect).
