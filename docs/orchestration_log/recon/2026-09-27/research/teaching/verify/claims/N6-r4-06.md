# N6-r4-06 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
A randomized controlled trial (n=88 adults meeting DSM-IV criteria for ADHD) found a 12-week manualized group program explicitly teaching time-management, organization and planning skills ("Meta-Cognitive Therapy") produced significantly greater improvement in independent-evaluator-rated inattentive symptoms than an active supportive-therapy control matched for group format and medication status (odds ratio 5.41, 95% CI 1.77-16.55, for a defined responder threshold); outcomes were assessed only immediately pre/post-treatment, with no reported follow-up. DOI 10.1176/appi.ajp.2009.09081123.

## Primary text (quote, location)
Full text read (PMC author manuscript, `https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3633586/`; Am J Psychiatry 2010;167(8):958–968). Abstract/Method/Results:

> "Objective: To examine the efficacy of a 12-week manualized Meta-Cognitive Therapy (MCT) group designed to enhance time-management, organization, and planning in adults with AD/HD. Method: Eighty-eight clinically referred adults who met DSM-IV criteria for ADHD based on clinical and structured diagnostic interviews and standardized questionnaires were stratified vis-à-vis ADHD medication use and otherwise randomly assigned to receive MCT or supportive psychotherapy in a group modality... Therapeutic response was assessed by an independent (blind) evaluator via structured interview pre- and post-treatment, as well as by self-report and collateral informant behavioral ratings. Results: ... Logistic regression examining group differences in operationally defined response (controlling for baseline ADHD severity) revealed a robust effect of Treatment Group (odds ratio=5.41; 95%CI=1.77,16.55)."

All design facts, n, randomization, stratification, control condition, instrument, and the exact odds ratio/CI match the claim.

## Grade (S, R, O — changed from survey? why)
Survey: S=3, R=2, O=1.

- **S = 3** — unchanged. Single RCT with an active control, matches S=3 exactly.
- **R = 2** — unchanged. Population is adults with a clinical diagnosis (ADHD) learning executive-function/organizational skills via group therapy — not CS-adjacent professional-skill acquisition (R=3), not K-12/lab (R=1); "other skill domain" (R=2) is the closest fit for a life-skills/executive-function intervention, though it is a looser match than R=2's listed examples (music, surgery, sport, language). Kept at R=2 rather than downgrading, since real skills (time-management, organization, planning) are the explicit target and are taught and measured, unlike N6-r4-05.
- **O = 1** — unchanged, but flagged as borderline. Outcome is not a skill-performance task; it is a clinician-rated (independent, blind) symptom-severity interview, supplemented by self-report/collateral ratings, assessed immediately post-treatment. This is more rigorous than pure self-report (independent blind evaluator) but is not "unaided performance" in the sense the O rubric implies (e.g., a task done without help). O=1 is kept as the closer of the two available categories, since it is not simple self-report/satisfaction/time-on-task (which would force O=0), but this is a symptom-rating outcome, not a task-performance outcome.
