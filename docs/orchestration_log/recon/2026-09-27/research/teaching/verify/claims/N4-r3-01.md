# N4-r3-01 — Verification

## Claim as surveyed
"A recurrent-neural-network diagnostic model (Deep Knowledge Tracing) predicted held-out student-response correctness far more accurately than the standard Bayesian Knowledge Tracing model used to diagnose learner mastery: AUC 0.85 vs 0.68 on Khan Academy data and 0.86 vs 0.69 on the Assistments dataset." (arxiv.org/abs/1506.05908)

## ID resolution
`export.arxiv.org/api/query?id_list=1506.05908` resolves to a single hit: "Deep Knowledge Tracing," Piech, Spencer, Huang, Ganguli, Sahami, Guibas, submitted 2015-06-19 — matches the stated attribution (Piech et al. 2015) exactly. OpenAlex record (`10.48550/arxiv.1506.05908`, W650350307): 627 citations, a landmark paper in the field (also published at NeurIPS 2015).

## Primary text (quote, location)
Full text fetched directly (`arxiv.org/pdf/1506.05908v1`, 13 pp.).

Results, p.6: "On the Khan dataset using a LSTM neural network model led to an AUC of 0.85 which was a notable improvement over the performance of a standard BKT (AUC = 0.68), especially when compared to the small improvement BKT provided over the marginal baseline (AUC = 0.63). See Table 1 and Figure 3(b). On the Assistments dataset the DKT had a 25% gain on the previous best reported result (AUC = 0.86 and 0.69 respectively) [23]."

Table 1 (p.5): `Khan Math ... Marginal 0.63, BKT 0.68, BKT* –, DKT 0.85` / `Assistments ... Marginal 0.62, BKT 0.67, BKT* 0.69, DKT 0.86`. Table caption: "BKT is the standard BKT. BKT* is the best reported result from the literature for Assistments."

**Discrepancy found**: the Khan Academy pair (0.85 vs 0.68) is exactly "DKT vs. standard BKT," matching the claim. The Assistments pair the claim cites (0.86 vs 0.69) is exactly what the paper's own running prose reports — but Table 1 shows this 0.69 is **BKT\*** ("the best reported result from the literature," an external, more-sophisticated baseline from a cited prior paper [23]), not the paper's own **standard BKT** run on Assistments, which scored **0.67**. The claim's label "the standard Bayesian Knowledge Tracing model" is accurate for the Khan Academy comparison but technically wrong for the Assistments comparison — the true standard-BKT gap on Assistments is 0.86 vs 0.67 (a larger gap than claimed), not 0.86 vs 0.69 (which is DKT vs. the best prior literature result, a stronger and different baseline).

## Replications & critics
A direct, substantive critique exists and is highly relevant: **Khajah, Lindsey & Mozer, "How deep is knowledge tracing?"** (arXiv:1604.02416, 2016) — confirmed resolving via arXiv API to that exact title/authors. Its abstract states: "when BKT is extended to allow it more flexibility in modeling statistical regularities... BKT achieves a level of performance indistinguishable from that of DKT... its gains do not come from the discovery of novel representations." This does **not** contradict the numbers quoted here (DKT vs. *standard* BKT) — it shows that *extended/tuned* BKT variants (not the "standard" BKT the original paper compares against) can close the gap. It is important context: the "far more accurately" framing holds specifically against the weak standard-BKT baseline, not against the strongest available Bayesian alternatives.

## Status: VERIFIED

The core reported numbers (0.85/0.68 Khan; 0.86/0.69 Assistments) are quoted exactly from the primary text and are accurate. Flagged: the claim's characterization of the Assistments 0.69 figure as "the standard Bayesian Knowledge Tracing model" is imprecise — Table 1 labels it BKT\* (best-in-literature, not the paper's own standard-BKT run, which scored 0.67). A subsequent independent reanalysis (Khajah et al. 2016) shows extended BKT variants can match DKT, which should temper any "DKT strictly dominates Bayesian methods" reading of this claim.
