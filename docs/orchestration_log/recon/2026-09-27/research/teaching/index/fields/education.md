## Disputes

- #4/#6/#26 (Sweller cognitive load line) vs. #7 (Kirschner/Sweller/Clark) together vs. #17 (Kapur, productive failure): direct-instruction-first (worked examples reduce load, minimal guidance fails for novices) versus struggle-before-instruction (productive failure benefits from unsupported initial attempts) — same evidence base (cognitive load, novice working memory), opposite prescriptions; not resolved by either side's cited studies alone. Flagged for Tier 4 per the plan's named standing dispute ("worked examples vs productive failure").
- #29 (Kulik & Kulik 1988: immediate feedback wins in applied settings) vs. classic list-learning results the same paper cites as showing delayed feedback wins in verbal-lab tasks: the meta-analysis itself reports the reversal by setting, not a contradiction between two anchors — recorded here because a future searcher will otherwise assume one direction of the "immediate vs. delayed feedback" question is settled.
- #14 (Soderstrom & Bjork: performance during training misleads about learning) is the interpretive frame that #21's finding (delayed test-like feedback beats immediate) and #27's field RCT (interleaving costs performance now, pays off on delayed test) both instantiate; not a disagreement, but three anchors making the same underlying point from different data — noted so a synthesizer does not triple-count it as convergent independent evidence.
