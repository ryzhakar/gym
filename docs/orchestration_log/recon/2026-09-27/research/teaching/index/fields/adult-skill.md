## Disputes

- **Deliberate practice's explanatory power.** Ericsson, Krampe & Tesch-Römer (1993, #21) and Ericsson (2004, 2015, #22/#23) argue deliberate practice is the dominant driver of expert performance, including in medicine. Macnamara, Hambrick & Oswald (2014, #24), meta-analyzing the same construct across domains, find deliberate practice explains a moderate share of variance in games and music but a much smaller share in professions — directly disputing the strength (not the existence) of the effect Ericsson's papers claim.
- **Whether CRM training demonstrably works.** Salas et al. (2001, #28) frame CRM evidence as consistently positive at the reaction/learning level. Five years later, O'Connor et al. (2006, #29), reviewing an expanded set of the same kind of studies, reach a more cautious verdict — evidence is still concentrated at reaction/attitude level, with behavior- and organizational-outcome evidence remaining thin — and issue an explicit call for stronger outcome designs, unresolved as of that update.
- **Skill-decay curve and its moderators.** Arthur, Bennett, Stanush & McNelly (1998, #42) established the classic quantitative retention-decay curve (steeper for cognitive vs. physical/procedural tasks, moderated by overlearning and retention interval). Tatel & Ackerman (2025, #43), meta-analyzing procedural skill retention specifically with a much larger and more recent evidence base, revise several of those moderators — flagged here as an update/partial revision rather than a flat contradiction, since #43 narrows scope to procedural skills where #42 was domain-general.
