## Disputes

1. **Does deliberate practice explain most of expert skill, or a modest fraction?** #1 (Ericsson et al. 1993, ~10-year/effortful-practice account, implicitly near-total explanatory role) vs. #2/#3/#4 (Macnamara/Hambrick meta-analyses: 26% down to <1% of variance depending on domain) vs. #10 (Hambrick et al. defend the meta-analytic reading) — an active, still-unresolved effect-size dispute, not a resolved consensus.
2. **Is starting age/domain-specific practice sufficient, or do other factors dominate?** #6 and #24 (Campitelli & Gobet; Gobet & Campitelli — starting age matters independently) vs. #1/#12/#14 (Ericsson-tradition studies attributing skill primarily to practice quantity/quality) — same underlying debate, chess-domain evidence on both sides.
3. **Power law vs. exponential law of practice.** #32 (Newell & Rosenbloom 1981 — power law) vs. #33 (Heathcote, Brown & Mewhort 2000 — power law is an averaging artifact; true individual curves are exponential) vs. #34 (Evans et al. 2018 — refines but does not fully resolve the disagreement). A direct, named empirical dispute over the functional form of learning-rate regularity.
4. **Does skill/cognitive training transfer far from the trained task?** #37 (Sala & Gobet 2017 — far transfer essentially does not exist) vs. #36 (Melby-Lervåg & Hulme 2013 — some transfer effects detected, though weak and inconsistent for far transfer) vs. #38 (Simons et al. 2016 — commercial far-transfer claims specifically are not supported). Directional agreement that far transfer is weak/absent, but #36 is more equivocal than #37's stronger negative claim — flagged as a dispute rather than folded into one line.
5. **Grit as an independent predictor of sustained skill.** #43 (Duckworth et al. 2007 — grit predicts achievement above and beyond known traits) vs. #44 (Credé, Tynan & Harms 2017 — meta-analysis finds grit's incremental validity over conscientiousness is small) — direct empirical contradiction on whether grit is a genuinely separate construct relevant to who sustains deliberate practice long enough to reach expertise.
6. **Expertise as pure benefit vs. also a source of rigidity.** #22/#23 (chunking/template theory — expertise as strictly superior pattern encoding) vs. #25 (Bilalić et al. 2008 — expertise also produces Einstellung rigidity, a documented cost) — not a citation-level contradiction but a substantive tension the index-user should not smooth over.
