## Disagreements between sources

- Diagnostic-accuracy ceiling is task-dependent, not a single number: N4-r3-01 shows large
  gains on *correctness* diagnosis (AUC), while N4-r3-03 shows the same model family is far
  weaker on *concept-level* diagnosis (Macro-F1) — these are not competing claims about one
  quantity, but two different diagnostic targets that should not be collapsed into one
  "LLM diagnostic accuracy" figure.
- N4-r3-04 (zero-shot LLM misconception classification underperforms keyword matching) and
  N4-r3-07 (fine-tuned LLM reaches human-tutor parity at behavior synthesis) look contradictory
  at a glance but are not: one is zero-shot classification over 546 labels, the other is
  fine-tuned generation on a 36-scenario benchmark with 6 labels — label-space size and
  fine-tuning both plausibly explain the gap, not measured against each other directly in
  either paper.

## Out of scope, seen

- The physician diagnostic-reasoning literature (Croskerry 2003 "cognitive errors in
  diagnosis"; premature-closure/cognitive-bias reviews found via Crossref and PMC) diagnoses a
  *patient's condition*, not a *learner's knowledge state* — a structurally different
  diagnostic task despite the shared vocabulary ("diagnosis," "cognitive bias"). Not used as
  N4 evidence; flagged here in case N5 (elite margin) or a future round wants the
  naturalistic-decision-making angle on expert judgment under uncertainty.
- "The Metacognitive Monitoring Battery" and "The Metacognitive Probe" (arXiv, found via the
  calibration search) benchmark an *LLM's own* self-calibration, not the LLM diagnosing a
  *learner's* calibration — relevant to N7 (what an LLM substrate can/can't do), not N4.
- "User Misconceptions of LLM-Based Conversational Programming Assistants" (arXiv 2510.25662)
  is about programmers' misconceptions *of the AI tool*, the reverse direction from N4's
  question.
