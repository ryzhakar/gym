## Disagreements between sources

- N7-r4-02 (self-report: reflective LLM engagement → higher perceived competence/retention) and
  N7-r4-07/08 (controlled: self-explanation prompting → no measured gain-score effect) point in
  different directions on the same underlying question (does prompting deeper engagement with an LLM's
  output help learning). The self-report study cannot separate engagement style as cause from engagement
  style as symptom of a already-more-competent learner (reverse causation); the RCT/quasi-experiment
  directly manipulated the prompts and training and found no effect. Weighed by S and O, the controlled
  null (S3, O1) should carry more weight than the cross-sectional self-report (S1, O0).
- N7-r4-06 (facilitative peer-tutor dialogue helps, constructive-type dialogue hurts) sits in tension
  with the general learning-sciences preference for constructive/generative activity; the source's own
  population is K-12 peers, not adults or an LLM-as-tutor context, so this may be a dialogue-taxonomy
  artifact (their "constructive" label may denote something closer to giving away the answer) rather
  than a genuine contradiction — flagged, not resolved, at survey grade.
