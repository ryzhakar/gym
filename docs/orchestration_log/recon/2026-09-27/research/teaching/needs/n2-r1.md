## Disagreements between sources

- **Lab-to-field shrinkage, same research program.** Margulieux, Catrambone & Guzdial (2016,
  10.1080/08993408.2016.1144429) found subgoal-unit instruction raised both immediate and
  1-week-delayed transfer performance in a controlled lab RCT (n=40). Margulieux, Morrison &
  Decker (2020, 10.1186/s40594-020-00222-7), scaling the same subgoal-labeling idea to a
  semester-long CS1 course (n=265), found the gain held on formative quizzes but not on summative
  exams — the O2-grade transfer effect from the lab study did not clearly reproduce at O1-grade
  course scale. Not a contradiction of mechanism, but a scale/outcome-type boundary the lab study
  alone would not reveal.
- **Productive failure's own boundary conditions, not yet reconciled with the index's standing
  cognitive-load dispute.** Sinha & Kapur (2021, 10.3102/00346543211019105) report the PS-I
  advantage reverses for domain-general skills and grades 2–5 — a partial, evidence-based
  explanation for (not a resolution of) the standing dispute the merged index already flags
  between the cognitive-load-theory line (Kirschner/Sweller/Clark 2006; Sweller/Cooper 1985) and
  Kapur's own productive-failure line (see `index/index.md`, Disputes, [education]). This round
  did not independently re-read the cognitive-load side (both candidate DOIs were paywalled — see
  below), so the dispute is reported, not adjudicated, here.
- **Interpretive recommendation vs. tested intervention.** The "trace before you write" sequencing
  claim (N2-r1-11, Lister et al. 2004) is the working group's own stated belief in a Discussion
  section, drawn from a correlational multi-institution finding, not from a randomized sequencing
  experiment — flagged so it is not read as equivalent in strength to the RCT-based claims above it
  in the same table.
