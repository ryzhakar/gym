## Disagreements between sources (pairs, one line)

- **Contextual-interference effect, existence and size.** Czyż, Wójcik & Solarská 2024
  (`10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1377122`, transfer) and Czyż, Wójcik, Solarská & Kiper 2024
  (`10.1038/s41598-024-65753-3`, retention) — both read in full — find medium-to-large,
  statistically significant benefits of high-CI (interleaved) over blocked practice, moderated by
  age and setting. Ammar et al. 2023 (`10.1016/j.edurev.2023.100537`) is characterized by the
  Czyż team's own introduction as concluding the effect is "a myth" (non-significant, small). I did
  not read Ammar directly (no abstract or OA text located this round) — this is reported as an
  unread, disputed claim, not a verified contradiction. The Czyż team also alleges Ammar's review
  skipped a major database (EBSCO) and was not preregistered; I have not verified that allegation
  either. Flagged for Tier 4.
- **Setting as a possible confound for age, not a clean moderator.** Within the retention paper
  itself (`10.1038/s41598-024-65753-3`), the age-subgroup finding (older adults show the largest
  CI benefit) and the setting-subgroup finding (lab shows large effects, applied settings do not)
  are not independent: the paper states nearly all older-adult studies were lab-based and nearly
  all youth studies were applied. Recorded here, as the plan asks for a real disagreement, but this
  is the source's own methodological caveat about its own data, not a clash between two sources.
