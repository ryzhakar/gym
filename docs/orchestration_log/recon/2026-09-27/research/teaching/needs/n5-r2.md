## Disagreements between sources

- Bilalić et al.'s own finding is internally two-sided, not a clean "elite margin" story: higher
  Elo predicts *more* resistance to the Einstellung effect (N5-r2-01/03), yet under the specific
  fixation manipulation the margin briefly collapses to zero (N5-r2-02) -- the same paper supports
  both "expertise protects" and "expertise's protection is conditional and can be erased," and both
  readings rest on the same dataset, not competing studies.
- Christensen (2009) and Martindale, Collins & Abraham (2007) describe elite know-how in opposite
  registers: Christensen's coaches say their core judgment (talent-spotting) is inherently tacit and
  actively resists a shared explicit vocabulary (N5-r2-08); Martindale et al.'s coaches describe
  explicit, statable principles and methods (individualize everything, delegate ownership, hold a
  long-term philosophy) that were captured in words well enough to code into themes. Both are
  self-report from elite coaches in different sports/countries; the disagreement may reflect a real
  split between an articulable-strategy layer and a tacit-perception layer of elite coaching
  expertise, not a contradiction to be resolved by picking one study.
- The index's own entries (not independently read this round, see below) report that deliberate
  practice quantity explains shrinking variance in more elite/competitive samples (Macnamara,
  Moreau & Hambrick 2016, 10.1177/1745691616635591: "effect smaller for more competitive/elite
  samples") -- if that index summary holds up on a future full-text read, it would sit in tension
  with a pure quantity-of-practice account of this need's own question and would support this
  round's behavioral/qualitative claims (which locate the margin in *what* elite coaches do, not
  in accumulated hours). Flagged here as a disagreement to verify, not asserted as read evidence.
