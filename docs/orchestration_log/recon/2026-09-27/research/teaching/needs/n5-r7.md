## Disagreements between sources (pairs, one line)

- N5-r7-02 (jtpe.2018-0292) vs. the broader deliberate-practice literature already in the index (Macnamara et al. 2014, `10.1177/0956797614535810`, deliberate practice explains only 4% of variance in "education" domains): the PE-teaching case found a workshop closed most of the expert/non-expert gap on a specific, bounded content-knowledge deficit, while the domain-general meta-analysis finds practice/training explains little variance in education outcomes overall — the disagreement is about scope (a narrow, diagnosable knowledge gap vs. general teaching skill), not a direct contradiction.
- N5-r7-03 (Höfer, expert pairs slower but higher-coverage) vs. the everyday assumption (and much of the deliberate-practice literature's framing) that "expert" performance is faster: this source's own finding is internally two-directional, expert pairs are *better* on one dimension (coverage) and *worse* on another (speed) — flagged here so the synthesizer does not read "expert margin" as uniformly positive.
