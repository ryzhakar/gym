## Disagreements between sources

- N6-r5-01/02 (autonomy-support and intrinsic engagement predict sustained, low-dropout behavior) sits in tension with N6-r5-10 (flow — the paradigm case of intrinsically engaging activity — coexists with *lower* self-reported motivation than leisure): good in-the-moment experience does not straightforwardly convert into a felt pull to keep doing the activity; the two literatures answer different questions (motivation *quality*/internalization vs. moment-to-moment *desire*) and should not be read as contradicting, but a synthesis that treats "make it flow-like" as sufficient for sustained voluntary return would be over-reading N6-r5-01/02.
- N6-r5-07 (extrinsic gamification — points/portal mechanics — measurably sustains software professionals' learning behavior over a year) sits against the Self-Determination Theory line in N6-r5-01/02 and the broader CET literature (not re-read this round, established in prior N6/education rounds) that externally imposed reward structures can crowd out intrinsic motivation; TechMark reports no motivation-quality measure (only behavioral outcomes) and no test of what happens after the portal is withdrawn, so the two findings are not a direct clash, but a full synthesis needs both.
