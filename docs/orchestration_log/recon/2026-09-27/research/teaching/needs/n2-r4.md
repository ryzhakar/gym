## Disagreements between sources

- Adjei, Botelho & Heffernan 2016 (data-driven prerequisite strength "largely confirm[ed]" by teacher judgment) sits in tension with the AAAI 2024 paper's own limitations note (N2-r4-08) that strict prerequisite-mastery ordering does not always hold in real student trajectories -- one paper's headline finding is the other's flagged caveat. Not a direct contradiction (different datasets, different claims: agreement-with-experts vs an ordering assumption's known failure mode), so not escalated to Tier 4, but the two are worth reading together.
- The three theory/framework papers on 4C/ID for programming/computational-thinking (N2-r4-03, N2-r4-04, N2-r4-05) all assert transfer benefits of whole-task-plus-part-task sequencing but none reports a learner outcome; the only outcome-bearing test of a part/whole ordering choice found this round (N2-r4-01/02, medical procedural skill) favored the opposite structure from what "whole-task-first" framing implies -- it taught in two discrete parts before assembling the whole, and beat teaching the whole procedure at once. The theory papers and the one RCT are not measuring the same variable (4C/ID's "whole task" means a simplified authentic whole version of the target task done early, not "teach the entire complex procedure uninterrupted") and are not a contradiction on inspection, but a reader skimming both could wrongly read them as opposed. Flagging for Tier 4/5 attention if either becomes load-bearing.
