## Disagreements between sources

- Ericsson et al. 1993 (N3-r7-10) frames a decade of *effortful, structured* practice as the near-total driver of durable expert performance; the ledger already carries three later meta-analyses (Macnamara et al. 2014, 10.1177/0956797614535810; Macnamara et al. 2016, 10.1177/1745691616635591; and the corrigendum) finding deliberate practice explains a much smaller share of variance (4–26% depending on domain) — not contradictory data so much as a 20-year downward revision of the same construct's explanatory power. Not re-verified against primary text this round (already ledgered from prior rounds); flagged here because it directly qualifies how much weight N3-r7-10 should carry.
- Kluger & DeNisi 1996 (N3-r7-05) and Hattie & Timperley 2007 / Shute 2008 (N3-r7-06/07) agree on the core mechanism (feedback's effect depends on what it directs attention to / its content and specificity, not on its mere presence) but neither this round's abstracts nor the existing ledger entries pin down a single universal answer on feedback *timing* (immediate vs. delayed) — Shute's abstract explicitly states timing effects "interact with... variables" rather than resolving to one rule, and this round could not independently read Bangert-Drowns et al. 1991 or Kulik & Kulik 1988 (both paywalled, WebSearch budget exhausted this session) to settle it further.
