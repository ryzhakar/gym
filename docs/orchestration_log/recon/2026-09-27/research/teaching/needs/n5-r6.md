## Disagreements between sources (pairs, one line)

- N5-r6-08 (Webster 2010, one-to-one adult golf lessons: expert vs. novice instruction produced statistically indistinguishable 2-week recall) vs. the classroom-teacher expert-novice literature already in the ledger (O'Connor/Fish/Yasik family; prior N5 rounds' teacher-noticing studies), which finds real expert-novice divergence in what is attended to and communicated. Webster's own discussion attributes the gap to the one-to-one lesson ratio and the specific nonverbal channel studied, not to a refutation of expert-novice differences generally — a moderator candidate (setting: 1:1 vs. classroom; population: adult vs. K-12) rather than a flat contradiction.
- N5-r6-04 (JAMA: AI feedback beat the *specific* remote human expert instructor on objective metrics, though holistic OSATS ratings tied) sits in tension with the "elite margin exists and matters" framing this need investigates: here, an ordinarily-competent expert instructor's practice-phase feedback was not detectably better than no feedback at all on the objective metric, while a system built without any modeled human expertise (an ML classifier over simulator telemetry) outperformed both. This is evidence that *not all deployed "expert instruction" reaches its own literature's documented elite margin* — the margin in N5-r6-01/02/03/05 is not free, it requires specifically encoding the differentiating behavior (gaze, dialogue moves), not just recruiting a credentialed expert.
