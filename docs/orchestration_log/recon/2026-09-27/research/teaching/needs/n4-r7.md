## Disagreements between sources

- Calibration improvability: Hacker et al. (2008) find lower-performing students' calibration
  *does* improve, but only under extrinsic incentives — while Händel & Dresel (2018) find
  low-performers' *second-order* judgments stay miscalibrated even when first-order confidence is
  appropriately low. Not a direct contradiction (different judgment layers, different populations
  and 10 years apart) but the pairing shows calibration accuracy is fixable on one measure and
  stubborn on another within the same broad phenomenon — flagged, not resolved, here.
- Tannert et al. (2026) find that *explicitly telling* a diagnosing adult which cues are
  non-diagnostic does not restore judgment accuracy; this sits in tension with the general
  feedback-design literature's assumption (seen elsewhere in the index, not re-litigated here)
  that specific corrective information reliably improves performance — worth a Tier-4 look if this
  claim becomes load-bearing.
