## Disagreements between sources (pairs, one line)

- 10.7771/1541-5015.1046 (Strobel & van Barneveld) vs. 10.1016/j.edurev.2018.11.001 (Chen & Yang): the meta-synthesis reports traditional/drill beating PBL on short-term, standardized-exam retention while PBL wins long-term; the later meta-analysis reports an overall positive PBL effect (d+=0.71) without splitting by retention delay in the abstract — the two cannot both be read as "PBL simply wins" without knowing which outcome-delay band the second study's pooled effect sits in; unresolved without their primary texts (both full-text-blocked this round: Strobel by a Cloudflare challenge, Chen & Yang not OA).
- 10.3102/00346543060002265 (Kulik et al., mastery learning: practice-to-criterion, drill-like) and Kulik's positive short-term exam effect sit closer to Strobel's "traditional wins short-term" pole, while 10.1080/01443410.2015.1051949 (Blayney et al.) and 10.1007/s11251-012-9237-2 (Rey & Fischer) show the opposite pattern is possible — decomposed/explanatory practice helping only specific learners (novices, or only on transfer) — suggesting "drill vs. project" is not one axis but crossed with a prior-knowledge moderator neither meta-analysis's abstract reports controlling for.
