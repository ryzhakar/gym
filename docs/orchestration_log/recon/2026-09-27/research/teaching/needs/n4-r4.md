## Disagreements between sources

- **[N4, new this round]** McArthur, Stasz & Zmuidzinas (1990) — direct observation of expert
  human tutors finds almost no fine-grained misconception diagnosis and shows tutor behavior is
  largely insensitive to the specific cause of a student error — versus the entire cluster of
  AI-tutoring systems already logged under N4 in r2/r3 (BKT/DKT knowledge tracing, LLM
  misconception classifiers, generate-retrieve-rerank misconception diagnosis) whose designs
  assume fine-grained misconception diagnosis is the central, valuable mechanism. Not a
  head-to-head empirical contradiction (different eras, different substrates) but a real tension
  worth flagging for Tier 4: the human-tutoring evidence base does not obviously support
  diagnosis-centric design as "what expert humans do," even though it is what most AI-tutoring
  systems are built to do.
- **[N4]** Kruger & Dunning (1999) vs. Krueger (2013) "signal extraction" counter-explanation of
  the Dunning-Kruger effect — a documented critique pair, but neither DOI's abstract or full text
  was reachable this round (publisher-elided abstracts on both). Logged as a disagreement pair by
  title only; **not** independently verified, so no claim was drawn from either.
