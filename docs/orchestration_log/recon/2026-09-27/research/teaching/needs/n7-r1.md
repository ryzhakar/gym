## Disagreements between sources (pairs, one line)

- N7-r1-01/02 (Bastani et al., ungated harm / guardrailed near-null) vs. N7-r1-03/04/05/07 (Kestin,
  Wang, Sun & Liu, Contractor & Reyes — all report AI-tutoring gains): not a real contradiction — the
  plausible moderator is guardrail/structure design and whether the practice phase substitutes for or
  scaffolds the learner's own reasoning; already flagged as the field's own standing dispute in
  `index/index.md`'s `[ai-tutoring]` disputes section (#17 vs #16).
- N7-r1-10 (Kosmyna et al., EEG cognitive-debt harm) vs. its own published critique (arXiv
  `10.48550/arxiv.2601.00856`, Stanković et al.): a direct methodological dispute over the
  EEG-connectivity inference, already flagged in the merged index; not independently re-adjudicated
  here (that is Tier 3/4's job).
- N7-r1-05 (single RCT, n=124: AI tutor ties human instructor) vs. N7-r1-06 (4-trial meta-analysis,
  n=268: small, low-certainty AI edge, cognitive-load cost, hybrid recommended): the meta-analysis is
  the stronger read (S4 vs S3) and narrows the single trial's tie-finding to "a small and uncertain
  edge with a cost," not a flat contradiction.
- N7-r1-07 (assisted gains persist at 1 week, undergrad topic-learning with AI access retained through
  the study) vs. N7-r1-08/09 (full-delegation coding assistance and on-demand logic-puzzle assistance
  both leave unaided skill worse once assistance is unavailable): plausible moderator is whether AI use
  during practice was delegation (do the task for me) or tutoring (helps me do the task) — the
  Contractor & Reyes design gives learners AI access as a learning aid on a topic being newly taught;
  Shen & Tamkin and Wu/Belem et al. measure what happens after help is withdrawn on a task the learner
  was meant to internalize. Flagged for Tier 4, not resolved here.
