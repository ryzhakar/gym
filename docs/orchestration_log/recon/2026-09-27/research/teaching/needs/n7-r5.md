## Disagreements between sources (pairs, one line)

- Whether an AI tutor matches or beats a human tutor on skill gained is domain-dependent, not settled: the already-indexed Springer RCT (10.1007/s10639-025-13779-z, N=124, laparoscopic curriculum, delayed OSATS post-test) found **no significant difference** between AI and human tutor groups, while this round's BJS-abstract RCT (10.1093/bjs/znad241.142, N=98, simulated brain-tumor resection, within-session repeated trials) found the AI arm **significantly ahead** of the human arm by the final repetition — different tasks, different outcome timing (delayed vs within-session), both surgical-simulation, same general question.
- The same "AI vs human tutor" question runs the other way in a non-medical domain: icobits's EFL quasi-experiment (10.32664/icobits.v1.130) found the **human tutor produced higher grammar-accuracy gains** than the chatbot, the reverse ranking of the BJS finding above.
- Within the single ssrn.6297817 study, a designed guardrail intervention (adaptive metacognitive nudges) raised persistence but left process and performance unchanged — a partial-null result sitting next to SocraticAI's (10.48550/arxiv.2512.03501) descriptive claim that a guardrailed design shifted visible behavior (vague help-seeking → structured decomposition) in 75%+ of students; the two designs and evidence grades (S3 exploratory vs S1 descriptive) are not directly comparable, but both speak to whether guardrail nudges change learner behavior, with differently-shaped outcomes (motivation vs behavior pattern).
