## On the attention sub-question (line 120)

This round found no established **CS-specific** method for channeling or riding
hyperfocus-and-breadth attention — consistent with round 2's practitioner-thread finding
("notable absence... advice is individual coping, not a documented practice"). What it adds: a
**general, non-CS**, RCT-graded method for training attention/organization/planning skills in
adults does exist and shows an effect (N6-r4-06) — the gap is narrower than "nothing works,"
narrower still than "something CS-specific works." See Disagreements below.

## Disagreements between sources

- N6-r4-06 (Solanto et al., RCT, S3) vs. the round-2 HN-ADHD-threads finding (S1, practitioner
  discussion, already in `sources.csv`): the threads found no established *CS-specific* channeling
  method; Solanto's trial shows an established *general* (non-CS) method for training the
  underlying skills (time-management, organization, planning) does work at RCT grade. Not a
  contradiction — a population/specificity mismatch: general-adult evidence exists where
  CS-specific evidence does not. Flagged for Tier 4 only if a synthesiser needs the distinction
  resolved; no source here contradicts another on the same claim.
- N6-r4-05 (Thomas et al. 2016, context-change window closes within ~12–24 months) nuances rather
  than contradicts the existing N6-r3 "Fresh Start Effect" row (10.1287/mnsc.2014.1901): both agree
  a temporal/context break motivates behaviour change, but this round's source adds that the effect
  measurably *decays*, with the strongest gains in the earliest months — bearing on how soon after
  a re-entry (post-interruption resumption) a trainer's window of leverage narrows.
