## Disagreements between sources

- 10.1007/s10664-023-10411-x vs. the 2015 Microsoft study it replicates (10.1109/msr.2015.21, itself unread this round, CLOSED/no abstract): the 2015 study reportedly found reviewer file-specific familiarity, review-count on the file, and reviewer tenure all positively associated with useful reviews; the 2024 OpenDev study finds none of the first two significant and a *negative* tenure association — the two disagree on which experience measure matters, not on whether experience matters at all (general coding experience is positive in both).
- Ericsson-style deliberate-practice framing (N5-r4-08/09, applied here to forensic experts) implies a domain-general lever (feedback quality/frequency) drives continued expert improvement, while the OpenDev findings (N5-r4-01 to 06) locate the lever in structural/scheduling factors (rotation, load, delay) as much as in feedback content — these are not contradictory but sit at different levels of the same mechanism, and no single source here tests both together.
