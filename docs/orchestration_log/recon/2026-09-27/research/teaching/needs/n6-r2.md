## Disagreements between sources

- **Trait-level "curiosity/grit" vs. behavioral/environmental supports, as sustain mechanisms.** Ruling 120 asks whether CS-specific ways exist to *channel* curiosity/hyperfocus. The trait-level literature this round found (N6-r2-03, N6-r2-04) shows grit — and specifically its curiosity-adjacent facet, "consistency of interest" — is a weak-to-moderate predictor of sustained performance/retention, weaker than the "perseverance of effort" facet. Meanwhile the only CS-specific evidence on channeling (N6-r2-05, N6-r2-06, from Liebel et al. 2024) is about externally-scaffolded environmental/social strategies (body-doubling, task externalization, flexible work modes), not about cultivating curiosity as a trait. These point in different directions on what "channeling" should mean, and neither is O≥1 (no delayed unaided-performance outcome in either).
- **Hyperfocus named as an asset vs. as an unmanaged co-occurrence.** N6-r2-09 (Hupfeld et al.) establishes hyperfocus as a measurable, replicated trait correlated with ADHD symptomology, purely descriptively. N6-r2-06 (Liebel et al.) reports working engineers describing the same phenomenon as simultaneously useful and harmful, with no described method that reliably converts the liability into the asset. Not a contradiction, but worth flagging: no source found this round measures whether any intervention *changes* the hyperfocus-outcome relationship, only that the relationship exists and is felt.
