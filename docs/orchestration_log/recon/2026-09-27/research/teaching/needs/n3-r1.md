## Disagreements between sources (pairs, one line)

- **Kulik & Kulik 1988 vs. Shute 2008 on "when":** Kulik & Kulik report immediate feedback wins in
  applied classroom settings and delayed feedback sometimes wins in lab list-learning — an empirical
  split by setting, not a resolved single answer; Shute's design recommendations treat timing as one
  of several tunable parameters rather than picking a side. Not a contradiction, but the two sources
  together show "feedback timing" has no single correct value independent of task/setting.
- **Tutor CoPilot vs. Bastani et al. on "what to hand the learner":** Tutor CoPilot's real-time LLM
  suggestions go to the *human tutor*, who filters and delivers them, and raise mastery; Bastani's
  unrestricted GPT-4 goes *directly to the student* and raises assisted scores while lowering unaided
  exam scores. Surface reading ("AI help works" vs. "AI help harms") looks contradictory; the two
  studies differ on exactly the variable N3 is about — who the intervention's content passes through
  before reaching the learner — so this is a candidate for Tier 4, framed as "what moderates" rather
  than "which is right."
- **Worked-examples-first (Kalyuga's expertise reversal, Sweller-style guidance) vs. productive
  failure (Sinha & Kapur):** a known standing dispute the project's plan already flags for Tier 4
  (fable-plan.md §2 line 80, §4). N3's evidence here (both directions found, both graded S2/S4) does
  not resolve it; both claims are reported, not adjudicated, per ruling 110.
